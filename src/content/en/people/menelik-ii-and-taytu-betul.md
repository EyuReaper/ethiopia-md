# Emperor Menelik II and Empress Taytu Betul: Architects of Modern Ethiopia

Emperor Menelik II (ዳግማዊ ምኒልክ) ruled Ethiopia from 1889 until his death in 1913, after more than two decades as king of Shewa. [Britannica: Menilek II](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Menilek-II) His wife, Empress Taytu Betul (ጣይቱ ብጡል), was one of his closest advisers and, for a time during his final illness, the person who held most of his authority. [Library of Congress: Taytu Betul: The Cunning Empress of Ethiopia](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2022/03/taytu-betul-the-cunning-empress-of-ethiopia/) [Journal of African History: Harold G. Marcus, "The end of the reign of Menilek II" (1970)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s002185370001046x) Together they founded Addis Ababa, led the army that defeated Italy at Adwa in 1896 and started building the institutions of a modern state. Menelik also more than doubled the territory he ruled through military conquest in the south, and that part of his record is still debated in Ethiopia today. [Library of Congress Country Studies: Ethiopia, The Reign of Menelik II, 1889–1913](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm)

## From King of Shewa to Emperor

Menelik was born on 17 August 1844 (Nehase 12, 1836 E.C.) in Ankober, in Shewa. He was king of Shewa from 1865 to 1889 and became emperor of Ethiopia in 1889, after the death of Emperor Yohannes IV. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Menilek-II) Britannica credits him with defeating Italy at Adwa, expanding the empire, starting modern educational programs and building up the country's infrastructure. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Menilek-II)

## Taytu Betul

Taytu's birth date is uncertain, because birth dates were rarely recorded in 19th-century Ethiopia. _Women in World History_ places her birth in 1850 or 1851, probably at Mahdere Maryam in Begemder. [Encyclopedia.com (Women in World History): Taytu (c. 1850–1918)](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/taytu-c-1850-1918) The Library of Congress's Ethiopia specialist gives 1853. [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2022/03/taytu-betul-the-cunning-empress-of-ethiopia/) She came from an aristocratic family, was educated in the church and is said to have composed _qene_, the traditional form of oral poetry. [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2022/03/taytu-betul-the-cunning-empress-of-ethiopia/)

She married five times; her last marriage, in April 1883, was to Menelik, then king of Shewa. She became queen of Shewa and, in 1889, empress of Ethiopia. They had no children. [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/taytu-c-1850-1918) According to the Library of Congress, she was crowned two days after Menelik with the title "Etege Taytu Betul, Light of Ethiopia", and the emperor consulted her before major decisions. [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2022/03/taytu-betul-the-cunning-empress-of-ethiopia/)

## Founding Addis Ababa

The new capital was built on a site Taytu chose in 1886. [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/taytu-c-1850-1918) The Library of Congress credits her with founding the city and giving it its name, Addis Ababa, "New Flower". [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2022/03/taytu-betul-the-cunning-empress-of-ethiopia/) [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm) The royal encampment there grew into a permanent capital during the 1890s, which helped bring in new ideas and technology. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm) Taytu also built the Entoto Maryam church and opened the city's first hotel, the Etege Taytu Hotel, which still stands. [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2022/03/taytu-betul-the-cunning-empress-of-ethiopia/)

## Wuchale and Adwa

In 1889 Menelik signed the Treaty of Wuchale with Italy. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm) The Italian and Amharic texts differed on whether the treaty made Ethiopia an Italian protectorate without control of its own foreign affairs. [History.com: How Ethiopia Beat Back Colonizers in the Battle of Adwa](https://www.history.com/news/battle-adwa-ethiopia-africa-colonization) The disputed clause was Article 17. The Library of Congress account says the discovery infuriated Taytu, and that she persuaded the emperor to go to war. [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2022/03/taytu-betul-the-cunning-empress-of-ethiopia/) Menelik renounced the treaty and repaid in full a loan Italy had made as part of it. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm)

Italian forces invaded Tigray in late 1895. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm) Menelik raised an army that History.com estimates at 80,000 to 120,000 men, drawn from almost all of Ethiopia's regions and ethnic groups, and marched north with Taytu. In January 1896 the Ethiopians besieged an Italian fort at Mekelle, forcing its surrender "in large part by implementing Taytu's strategy of cutting off the water supply". [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/battle-adwa-ethiopia-africa-colonization) _Women in World History_ credits her strategy against a fort at Adigrat instead, so accounts differ on the details. [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/taytu-c-1850-1918) At Adwa on 1 March 1896 (Yekatit 23, 1888 E.C.), she commanded her own contingent of about 5,000 troops and organized thousands of women to carry water and care for the wounded. [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2022/03/taytu-betul-the-cunning-empress-of-ethiopia/) [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/taytu-c-1850-1918)

The Italian army was routed. The peace that followed confirmed the annulment of the Wuchale treaty and Italy's recognition of Ethiopian independence, while Menelik let Italy keep its colony of Eritrea. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm) The battle itself is covered in detail in [the Battle of Adwa](/en/history/battle-of-adwa).

## Southern Expansion and a Contested Legacy

Menelik's reign was also a period of conquest. The Library of Congress country study describes a program of military expansion that more than doubled his domain: with superior firepower his forces overran the Kembata and Welamo regions and subdued Kefa and other Oromo- and Omotic-speaking peoples. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm) In the conquered south, land was treated as the emperor's to allocate to officials and soldiers, which left most of the local population as tenants with far fewer rights than peasants in the northern highlands. The study notes that this shaped how the south would respond to land reform after 1974. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm)

Ethiopians read this record in sharply different ways: the same reign that produced Adwa and the modern state also produced these conquests. This article reports the documented events rather than a verdict on them. By 1908 the European colonial powers had recognized Ethiopia's borders except with Italian Somaliland. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm)

## Building a Modern State

Menelik authorized a French company to build a railway from Addis Ababa to Djibouti; it was not completed until 1917. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm) The Bank of Abyssinia, Ethiopia's first modern bank, was set up under an agreement with the British-owned National Bank of Egypt and inaugurated by Menelik on 16 February 1906. [National Bank of Ethiopia: Our history](https://nbe.gov.et/about-us/our-history/) _Women in World History_ also lists new roads and bridges, tax reform, a national currency, a postal system and the promotion of secular education. [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/taytu-c-1850-1918)

As his health declined, Menelik moved to secure the state. The historian Harold Marcus writes that he set up a European-style cabinet and reformed the appellate courts. [Journal of African History](https://doi.org/10.1017/s002185370001046x) The country study dates the Council of Ministers to late 1907. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm)

## Illness, Succession and Taytu's Fall

Menelik suffered a disabling stroke in May 1906. In June 1908 he named his thirteen-year-old grandson, Lij Iyasu, as his heir, and after another stroke he appointed Ras Tessema Nadew as regent. [Library of Congress Country Studies](https://countrystudies.us/ethiopia/15.htm) Taytu opposed both choices. According to Marcus, she gathered more and more of the emperor's authority into her own hands and tried to build a power base in the north, until the Shewan nobility, who had much to lose, united against her and removed her from power in 1910. [Journal of African History](https://doi.org/10.1017/s002185370001046x)

Menelik died in Addis Ababa on 12 December 1913 (Tahsas 3, 1906 E.C.). [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Menilek-II) Marcus argues that, against the expectations of European diplomats, his empire did not break apart after him. [Journal of African History](https://doi.org/10.1017/s002185370001046x) Taytu died in Addis Ababa on 11 February 1918 (Yekatit 4, 1910 E.C.). [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/taytu-c-1850-1918)

The Obelisk of Axum, taken by Italy in the 20th-century occupation, was where Abebe Bikila made his winning move in Rome in 1960; see [Abebe Bikila and the marathon legacy](/en/people/abebe-bikila-marathon-legacy).

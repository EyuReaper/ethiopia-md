# The Azmari Tradition: Ethiopia's Minstrels of Wax and Gold

## 30-second overview

An azmari (አዝማሪ) is a professional minstrel who sings, often improvising, to the accompaniment of a one-stringed fiddle, the _masenqo_, or a lyre, the _krar_. Wikipedia describes azmari as male or female entertainers whose tradition comes from the Amhara highlands and compares them to medieval European minstrels and West African griots. [Wikipedia: Azmari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azmari) Their verse relies on double meanings called "wax and gold," and they have been court singers, social commentators, wedding performers and, for many years, people of low social standing. Scholars who have studied them describe a tradition that was marginalized under the empire and then recast after the 1974 revolution. [Damtew, ERJSSH 2017](https://ejol.aau.edu.et/index.php/ERJSSH/article/download/323/229/430) For the instruments, see [traditional instruments: krar and masenqo](/en/music/traditional-instruments-krar-masenqo).

## Who the azmari are

The word comes from Geez. A 2005 study in African Study Monographs by Itsushi Kawase notes that, following earlier scholars, "azmari" derives from _zammara_, meaning "he sang" or "one who praises God." Today it mostly refers to professional masenqo players and the female singers who accompany them. [Kawase, African Study Monographs, Suppl. 29, 2005](https://minpaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/313/files/KI_ASM%2029_137-142.pdf) Music in Africa agrees on the Geez derivation and says the term today mainly refers to venues where professional masenqo players perform with female singers. [Getachew, Music in Africa 2016](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/traditional-music-ethiopia/)

The word carries a social weight. Kawase reports that "azmari" carries a negative image among many Amharic speakers because of the profession's stigma, and can mean "one who talks too much." The performers in Gondar he studied prefer the term _Enzata_, a self-designation based on descent, and call outsiders who learn the craft _Buga_. [Kawase 2005](https://minpaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/313/files/KI_ASM%2029_137-142.pdf) Ebrahim Damtew, writing in 2017, adds that some performers prefer names such as _alemachawach_ ("worldly entertainer") or the court title _leqemeqas_, and that the singer Yirga Dubale publicly prefers "azmari" and its meaning "one who praises God." [Damtew 2017](https://ejol.aau.edu.et/index.php/ERJSSH/article/download/323/229/430)

## A tradition with deep roots

All sources agree that the tradition is old, though they differ on how old and on where it began. Damtew writes that "all agree, however, that the azmari tradition is as old as the Ethiopian state," and summarizes a recent study that points to a Byzantine ambassador's account of King Kaleb's court (AD 514 to 542), which describes the king surrounded by dignitaries and flute players. [Damtew 2017](https://ejol.aau.edu.et/index.php/ERJSSH/article/download/323/229/430) That is suggestive, not proof of azmari in the modern sense. Wikipedia gives the mid-15th century as the earliest documented mention. [Wikipedia: Azmari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azmari)

By the 16th century the evidence is firmer. Damtew cites Father Álvares, reported in 1520, saying azmari served Ethiopian emperors and their dignitaries along with the roving capital and were often rewarded with land, cattle and valued gifts. The Scottish traveler James Bruce noted several azmari communities in Gondar at the turn of the 18th century. [Damtew 2017](https://ejol.aau.edu.et/index.php/ERJSSH/article/download/323/229/430) Damtew also records that around 1770 a large group of azmari was executed on the orders of Ras Mikael Sehul, citing Bruce, as an example of the risks of criticizing the powerful. [Damtew 2017](https://ejol.aau.edu.et/index.php/ERJSSH/article/download/323/229/430) Wikipedia notes that between 1841 and 1843 Major William Cornwallis Harris recorded a female azmari's praise song for Sahle Selassie's court. [Wikipedia: Azmari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azmari)

## Wax and gold

The craft's best-known feature is _qene_, or _semna-worq_, "wax and gold." Kawase explains that the literal sense of the lyrics is the wax, and as the wax melts the hidden message, the gold, slowly emerges. The audience must listen carefully for the golden meaning. [Kawase 2005](https://minpaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/313/files/KI_ASM%2029_137-142.pdf) Damtew adds that the form lets the azmari praise, flatter and criticize with "a calculated amount of sarcasm, word play and ambiguity," and that the saying "a priest for your soul's salvation, an azmari for its entertainment" captured the popular view. [Damtew 2017](https://ejol.aau.edu.et/index.php/ERJSSH/article/download/323/229/430)

Improvisation is expected of the audience as well as the performer. Kawase reports that azmari accept spontaneous verses from listeners and set to a familiar melody rhyming text about people and events at hand, and that skill at improvisation matters as much as playing or voice. Frequent themes are love in allusive or idealized form and the past glories of heroes. [Kawase 2005](https://minpaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/313/files/KI_ASM%2029_137-142.pdf) The tension between praise and mockery is old: Falceto recalls that the beloved singer Tilahun Gessesse was jailed for about a week after the 1960 coup attempt over a song with a double meaning. [Afropop Worldwide, 2006](https://old.afropop.org/articles/francis-falceto-ethiopia-empire-and-revolution)

## Where the azmari perform

Kawase's fieldwork in Gondar, in northern Ethiopia, challenges the image of azmari as bar musicians only. They perform at baptisms and weddings, where they lead the couple in procession praising them, and during the Orthodox festivals of Timkat, Qidus Yohannes (the new year) and Meskel; on the new year, groups visit homes to bless people, though some people brush them aside as beggars. They also play at zar spirit-possession ceremonies and during the teff harvest. [Kawase 2005](https://minpaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/313/files/KI_ASM%2029_137-142.pdf) Many still perform in _bunna bet_, _tella bet_ and _tej bet_, the coffee, beer and honey-wine houses (see [tej and tella](/en/food/tej-and-tella)). [Kawase 2005](https://minpaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/313/files/KI_ASM%2029_137-142.pdf)

In Addis Ababa, Kawase observes that _azmaribet_, cabarets such as those in Kazanchis and Yohannes Sefer, flourished after the fall of the Derg in 1991, each azmari belonging exclusively to one venue, and that working in them was viewed as a sign of success that drew rural azmari to the city. [Kawase 2005](https://minpaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/313/files/KI_ASM%2029_137-142.pdf) Wikipedia says azmari today perform at weddings, hotels and _tejbet_, and credits them with introducing the popular tizita ballad form. [Wikipedia: Azmari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azmari)

## Status: from outcast to star

Damtew argues that under the empire azmari were among the despised segments of society even as they were important to national life. He reports that in the 19th century, as elites embraced modernization, the term took on negative connotations, and that under Haile Selassie the promotion of Western instruments and bands, which were more highly valued, exacerbated their marginalization. After 1974, he writes, the revolution granted equality regardless of occupation, the Derg promoted folk music as part of a "cultural revolutionary transformation," and the profession became a "socially-dignified sector"; a growing number of young people without azmari ancestry joined it. [Damtew 2017](https://ejol.aau.edu.et/index.php/ERJSSH/article/download/323/229/430) His research is based on North Gondar and may not apply everywhere. Kawase notes that the number of performers in Gondar is high and many come from a few villages, the largest of which supplies 64 percent of the city's azmari. [Kawase 2005](https://minpaku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/313/files/KI_ASM%2029_137-142.pdf)

## On record

The tradition has also been recorded. The Ethiopiques reissue series includes a volume titled _Tètchawèt! Urban Azmaris of the 90s_ (volume 2, 1998) and one marking the centennial of the first Ethiopian recordings, with the azmari Tèssèma Eshèté in 1910 (volume 27, 2010). [Wikipedia: Éthiopiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89thiopiques) See [the Ethiopiques series](/en/music/ethiopiques-series-falceto).

## Closing

The azmari are a profession that was both indispensable and looked down upon. Their craft is wit hidden under a surface meaning, and the scholarship suggests that its present dignity is recent.

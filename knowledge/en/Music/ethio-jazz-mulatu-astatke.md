# Ethio-Jazz and Mulatu Astatke: The Alchemical Fusion of Highland Modes

Ethio-jazz is the name the vibraphonist and composer Mulatu Astatke (ሙላቱ አስታጥቄ) gave to his blend of American jazz, Latin rhythm and the five-note modes of Ethiopian music. [National Geographic: Riffing with Mulatu Astatke, the king of Swinging Addis](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/mulatu-astatke-ethiopia-jazz-addis) He developed it during years of study and work in Britain and the United States, and brought it home to Addis Ababa in the later 1960s, during the lively period of nightlife and recording now called "Swinging Addis". [The Conversation: The father of Ethiopian jazz, Mulatu Astatke, remains a musician in motion](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579) [NPR: Jazz from the Horn of Africa: 'Ethiopiques'](https://www.npr.org/2006/07/16/5560216/jazz-from-the-horn-of-africa-ethiopiques) That scene was shut down after the 1974 revolution. Its recordings found a world audience decades later through a French CD reissue series, _Éthiopiques_, and through Jim Jarmusch's 2005 film _Broken Flowers_. [Afropop Worldwide: Mulatu Astatke's Journey to Find Himself](https://afropop.org/articles/mulatu-astatkes-journey-to-find-himself)

## From North Wales to Boston and New York

Mulatu was born in 1943. [Afropop Worldwide](https://afropop.org/articles/mulatu-astatkes-journey-to-find-himself) In 1956 his parents sent him to North Wales to study aeronautical engineering, but he took up the trumpet instead and enrolled at Trinity College of Music in London, where he heard Caribbean and West African musicians who reminded him of the big bands he had known at home. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579) He told Afropop that he studied classical music at Trinity but decided he would rather find out what kind of musician he should be, and turned to jazz. [Afropop Worldwide](https://afropop.org/articles/mulatu-astatkes-journey-to-find-himself)

He then went to Boston, where he became the first African student at what is now the Berklee College of Music. Sources disagree on the year he arrived: the ethnomusicologist Kay Kaufman Shelemay writes that he had enrolled by 1958, while National Geographic gives 1963. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579) [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/mulatu-astatke-ethiopia-jazz-addis) At Berklee he swapped the trumpet for the vibraphone. Shelemay places him in New York by 1960, where for more than six years he played in the jazz scene, worked with Latin musicians and made records. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579) Berklee awarded him an honorary doctorate in 2012. [Berklee: Eagles, Alison Krauss, Mulatu Astatke Receive Honorary Degrees at Commencement](https://college.berklee.edu/node/1044822)

## Modes, Harmony and "Yekermo Sew"

Music in the Ethiopian highlands is organized around a small set of pentatonic modes, called _qenet_ (ቅኝት). Four are usually named: _tizita_, _bati_, _ambassel_ and _anchihoye_. [Wikipedia: Qenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qenet) Mulatu's albums from the early 1970s fused American jazz and Cuban rhythms with pentatonic scales built on these four modes. [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/mulatu-astatke-ethiopia-jazz-addis) Shelemay describes the result as a combination of bebop and modal jazz with melodies and harmonies based in the Ethiopian modal system. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579)

His signature piece, "Yekermo Sew" ("A Man of Experience and Wisdom"), shows how the parts fit together. Composed after his return to Ethiopia, it takes its title from a traditional Christian New Year blessing in Amharic. It is set in _tizita_, the mode most closely associated with nostalgia for the past, but its melody quotes Horace Silver's jazz tune "Song for My Father", reshaping its phrasing and rhythm and extending its repeated bass figure. The original recording used a Fender Rhodes electric piano and a guitar fuzz pedal, both new sounds at the time. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579) National Geographic singles out his 1972 album _Mulatu of Ethiopia_ as a landmark of the style. [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/mulatu-astatke-ethiopia-jazz-addis)

## Swinging Addis and the Curfew

Mulatu was one musician in a wider scene. Francis Falceto, the French producer who later compiled its recordings, told NPR that most of this music was recorded in the 1960s and early 1970s under Emperor Haile Selassie, when Addis Ababa had a "swinging" nightlife comparable to London's. He also credits the roughly 4,000 American Peace Corps volunteers who came to Ethiopia in the 1960s with bringing records of rock, soul, rhythm and blues and jazz that local musicians quickly adapted. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2006/07/16/5560216/jazz-from-the-horn-of-africa-ethiopiques) Falceto's own entry point was a record by the singer Mahmoud Ahmed, who started out as a shoeshine boy before being recruited by the leading bands of the day. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2006/07/16/5560216/jazz-from-the-horn-of-africa-ethiopiques)

Many of the key records were released by Amha Eshete on his Amha Records label. After Haile Selassie was overthrown in 1974, the military government imposed a night curfew that, in Falceto's account, lasted from 1974 until 1991, and the nightclub culture where this music had grown disappeared. Amha Eshete left the country. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2006/07/16/5560216/jazz-from-the-horn-of-africa-ethiopiques) Mulatu did not emigrate in those years; he kept his home in Ethiopia and travelled abroad to perform and record. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579)

The tenor saxophonist Getatchew Mekurya, born in 1935, belonged to the same generation. He spent 32 years as lead saxophonist, teacher and bandleader of the Police Orchestra and was known for his _shellela_ saxophone, a style Falceto describes as modeled on the war cries soldiers once hurled at the enemy. From 2004 he toured with the Dutch punk band The Ex, playing more than 100 concerts and recording two albums with them, _Moa Anbessa_ (2006) and _Y'Anbessa Tezeta_ (2012). He died in 2016 at 81. [Music in Africa: Ethiopian saxophonist Getachew Mekuria passes on at 81](https://www.musicinafrica.net/node/14992)

## Éthiopiques, Broken Flowers and Hip-Hop

Falceto tracked down the Amha Records master tapes, which had been pressed in Athens, after Amha Eshete returned to Ethiopia in the early 1990s, and began reissuing them on CD through the Paris label Buda Musique. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2006/07/16/5560216/jazz-from-the-horn-of-africa-ethiopiques) The fourth volume of the _Éthiopiques_ series, released in 1998, collected Mulatu's Ethio-jazz recordings from 1969 to 1974. [Afropop Worldwide](https://afropop.org/articles/mulatu-astatkes-journey-to-find-himself) By 2006 the series had reached 21 CDs. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2006/07/16/5560216/jazz-from-the-horn-of-africa-ethiopiques)

The film that took the music furthest was _Broken Flowers_ (2005). Jarmusch has said he created the character of Winston, an Ethiopian immigrant neighbor played by Jeffrey Wright, so that he could use Mulatu's music as the soundtrack; Winston hands Bill Murray's character a CD of "travelling music", and "Yekermo Sew" accompanies the road trip that follows. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579) Hip-hop producers followed. Damian Marley sampled Mulatu's "Yegelle Tezeta" for "As We Enter", the opening track of _Distant Relatives_ (2010), his album with Nas. [Mail & Guardian: Relative collaboration](https://mg.co.za/friday/2010-08-20-relative-collaboration/) Mulatu also recorded with the acid-jazz group the Heliocentrics for the _Inspiration Information_ series. [Afropop Worldwide](https://afropop.org/articles/mulatu-astatkes-journey-to-find-himself)

## Mulatu Today

At home, Mulatu has passed Ethio-jazz on to younger players, many of whom perform at his club, the African Jazz Village, in Addis Ababa. He has also researched traditional music from far-flung regions of the country and played it on his radio broadcasts. [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/the-father-of-ethiopian-jazz-mulatu-astatke-remains-a-musician-in-motion-93579) In a January 2026 interview, at 82, he steered the conversation away from his own career and toward the contribution of Ethiopia's rural traditional musicians to music history, saying: "As an African, it is my duty to give the respect to the geniuses of Africa." [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/mulatu-astatke-ethiopia-jazz-addis)

The modes Ethio-jazz draws on are played on instruments much older than the vibraphone; see [traditional Ethiopian instruments](/en/music/traditional-instruments-krar-masenqo). Jazz also shaped the painter Skunder Boghossian, covered in [Ge'ez Illuminations and Modern Art](/en/art/ethiopian-illuminations-modern-art).

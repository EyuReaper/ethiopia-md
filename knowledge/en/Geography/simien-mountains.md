# The Simien Mountains: The Roof of Africa and High-Altitude Wildlife Sanctuary

The Simien Mountains (ሰሜን ተራሮች) rise on the northern edge of the main Ethiopian plateau, in the North Gondar area of the Amhara Region. They include Ras Dejen (ራስ ዳሽን), the highest point in Ethiopia. [UNESCO: Simien National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) Over millions of years, erosion has cut the plateau into jagged peaks, deep gorges and cliffs that drop some 1,500 meters. The park is the only home of the walia ibex and an important refuge for the Ethiopian wolf and the gelada. [UNESCO: Simien National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) It was inscribed as a World Heritage site in 1978 and spent 1996 to 2017 on the List of World Heritage in Danger. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/1683/) Its flagship animal has since become a warning sign. Walia ibex counts fell from 865 in 2015 to 306 in 2024. [Scholte et al., Oryx (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/severe-decline-of-the-only-remaining-population-of-walia-ibex-in-ethiopia-proposed-actions-and-recommended-recategorization-as-critically-endangered/93CD6532B724CCC686E82F434D367F32)

## Volcanic Origins and Eroded Escarpments

The Simien massif is built from the thick basalt lavas of the Ethiopian flood basalts, or "traps". Most of these erupted about 30 million years ago, in a burst of a million years or less. [Hofmann et al.: Timing of the Ethiopian flood basalt event (Nature, 1997)](https://www.nature.com/articles/39853) Rivers have since carved the lava plateau into the present landscape. According to UNESCO, some cliffs reach 1,500 meters in height, and the northern cliff wall runs for about 35 kilometers, with deep valleys bounding the massif to the north, east and south. [UNESCO: Simien National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/)

Ras Dejen is usually given as 4,550 meters. Published figures vary, though: an Italian military survey gave 4,620 meters and a 1970s triangulation survey gave 4,533 meters. [Wikipedia: Ras Dashen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Dashen)

## A Refuge for Highland Endemics

UNESCO places the park within the Afroalpine Centre of Plant Diversity and the Eastern Afromontane biodiversity hotspot. More than 20 large mammal species and more than 130 bird species occur there. Five small mammal species and 16 bird species found in the park are endemic to Ethiopia and/or Eritrea, and the cliffs support an important population of the lammergeier (bearded vulture). [UNESCO: Simien National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/)

**Walia ibex (_Capra walie_, ዋልያ).** This wild mountain goat lives only in the Simien Mountains, mainly on the cliffs, across a range of about 100 square kilometers. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) [Scholte et al. (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/severe-decline-of-the-only-remaining-population-of-walia-ibex-in-ethiopia-proposed-actions-and-recommended-recategorization-as-critically-endangered/93CD6532B724CCC686E82F434D367F32) It is a national symbol of Ethiopia. Its fortunes are covered in the conservation section below.

**Ethiopian wolf (_Canis simensis_, ቀይ ቀበሮ).** UNESCO calls it probably the rarest canid species in the world. The Ethiopian Wolf Conservation Programme estimates that about 500 survive across Ethiopia's highlands, threatened by habitat loss and disease. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) [Ethiopian Wolf Conservation Programme](https://www.ethiopianwolf.org/) The species takes its scientific name from Simien, but its most important population today is in the Bale Mountains. [UNESCO: Bale Mountains National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/111/)

**Gelada (_Theropithecus gelada_, ጭላዳ).** The gelada lives only in the Ethiopian highlands, at about 1,800 to 4,400 meters. It is the only living member of its genus and the only primate that feeds mainly on grass, which can make up to 90 percent of its diet. [Wikipedia: Gelada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelada) Geladas sleep on cliff faces and forage on the grasslands above them. Their society has several levels: small reproductive units combine into bands, and bands into larger communities. [Snyder-Mackler et al.: Defining Higher Levels in the Multilevel Societies of Geladas (International Journal of Primatology, 2012)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10764-012-9584-5)

## Afroalpine Plants

Above the tree line, the plateau is covered in afroalpine grassland and heath, the habitat that the wolf and gelada depend on. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) Its most striking plant is the giant lobelia (_Lobelia rhynchopetalum_, ጅብራ), which is endemic to Ethiopia and grows only in the Simien and Bale mountains. It forms a thick stem topped with a rosette of leaves, and with its flower spike it can reach about 6.5 meters. [Wikipedia: Lobelia rhynchopetalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobelia_rhynchopetalum)

## People, Grazing and the Danger List

Simien is not an empty wilderness. Sources date the national park's establishment to 1966 or 1969. [Scholte et al. (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/severe-decline-of-the-only-remaining-population-of-walia-ibex-in-ethiopia-proposed-actions-and-recommended-recategorization-as-critically-endangered/93CD6532B724CCC686E82F434D367F32) [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) It was set up in an inhabited area, and when it was inscribed, 80 percent of the park was under some form of human use. UNESCO lists settlement, cultivation and soil erosion (particularly around the village of Gich), fires in the tree-heather forest, and large numbers of livestock as the main threats. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/)

In 1996 the site was put on the List of World Heritage in Danger because of a new road across it, overgrazing, farm encroachment and falling numbers of walia ibex, Ethiopian wolves and other large mammals. On July 4, 2017, the World Heritage Committee, meeting in Krakow, removed it from the list. The Committee cited better management, measures to reduce overgrazing and visitor impact, a commitment to build an alternative road, and the stabilization of walia ibex and gelada populations. [UNESCO: Simien National Park no longer in danger](https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/1683/)

## The Walia Ibex Crisis

The recovery recognized in 2017 did not last. The park was created to protect what Scholte and colleagues describe as the last 200 walia ibex. A coordinated census by the Ethiopian Wildlife Conservation Authority and partners counted 865 animals in 2015, about 650 in 2019 to 2021 and 306 in 2024. More than 70 percent of the local residents, park staff and officials interviewed blamed poaching for food and medicine. They saw instability during the Covid-19 crisis and the 2021 to 2022 war as the underlying cause. [Scholte et al. (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/severe-decline-of-the-only-remaining-population-of-walia-ibex-in-ethiopia-proposed-actions-and-recommended-recategorization-as-critically-endangered/93CD6532B724CCC686E82F434D367F32)

Later counts found 289 animals in November 2024 and 271 in December 2025. In 2026 the IUCN reassessment moved the species from Vulnerable back to Critically Endangered, because fewer than 250 mature animals remained. [Mongabay: Ethiopia's iconic Walia ibex is critically endangered once again](https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/2026/07/ethiopias-iconic-walia-ibex-is-critically-endangered-once-again/) The researchers propose a species action plan that relies on local community ambassadors and stronger protection. [Scholte et al. (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/severe-decline-of-the-only-remaining-population-of-walia-ibex-in-ethiopia-proposed-actions-and-recommended-recategorization-as-critically-endangered/93CD6532B724CCC686E82F434D367F32)

Simien remains one of the two main national parks protecting Ethiopian wolves, alongside Bale, and tourism built on its endemic wildlife is a source of local income. [Ethiopian Wolf Conservation Programme: Afroalpine Ecosystem](https://www.ethiopianwolf.org/the-wolves/afroalpine-ecosystem)

For the rift that splits the plateau on which Simien stands, see [the Great Rift Valley](/en/geography/great-rift-valley). For a wider look at Ethiopia's endemic species, see [Endemic Wildlife of Ethiopia](/en/nature/ethiopian-endemic-wildlife).

# The Danakil Depression and Dallol: A Rift Basin Below Sea Level

**In 30 seconds.** In the north of Ethiopia's Afar Region, the continental crust is being stretched apart, and the floor of the Danakil Depression (ዳናኪል) has sunk to roughly 100 to 125 m below sea level. It is a place of salt flats, volcanoes and acid hot springs, and one of the hottest places on Earth by annual average temperature. For generations Afar caravans have cut salt there by hand. Scientists study it as a window on how rifts open and on where microbial life stops.

## Where it is

The Danakil Depression is a valley of roughly 200 by 50 km across the north of the Afar Region of Ethiopia and Eritrea. It lies about 125 m (410 ft) below sea level, is bordered on the west by the Ethiopian Plateau and on the east by the Danakil Alps, beyond which lies the Red Sea, and is described as the third lowest-lying location in Africa. [Wikipedia: Danakil Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Depression) Depth figures differ by source and by the exact spot measured: the Wikipedia article quotes about 100 m for the salt plain in one place and 125 m for the depression elsewhere, and a geology reference gives 269 ft (about 82 m) below sea level for the Dallol site itself. [Geology.com: Dallol Volcano and Hydrothermal Field](https://geology.com/stories/13/dallol)

## How it formed

The depression is a minor structure in the rift between Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. As the rift opens, the floor subsides; after millions of years the deepest part sits at around 410 ft below sea level. [Geology.com](https://geology.com/stories/13/dallol) It sits within the wider Afar rift system; for that larger setting see [the Great Rift Valley](/en/geography/great-rift-valley).

The basin has been flooded from the Red Sea more than once. According to a geological summary on Wikipedia, this happened at least four times during the Mid and Late Pleistocene, and the evaporation of that water left evaporite beds more than 500 m thick, made largely of halite (rock salt) with potash minerals in the lower part. [Wikipedia: Danakil Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danakil_Depression) Geology.com adds that some deposits formed from evaporating runoff and hydrothermal brines. [Geology.com](https://geology.com/stories/13/dallol)

The rifting continues in real time elsewhere in the Afar. Between 14 September and 4 October 2005, 163 earthquakes of magnitude above 3.9 and a small eruption occurred along the roughly 60 km Dabbahu segment of the Afar rift. Satellite radar data showed the entire segment ruptured, with the crust opening by as much as 8 m, and about 2.5 cubic km of magma injected as a dyke; the authors called it the largest such episode on land in the era of satellite geodesy. [Wright et al., Nature (2006): Magma-maintained rift segmentation at continental rupture in the 2005 Afar dyking episode](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04978)

## Erta Ale and Dallol

Two volcanic features draw most attention.

**Erta Ale** ("smoking mountain" in Afar) is a basaltic shield volcano 613 m high in the south of the depression, with one or sometimes two active lava lakes in its summit pits. It is reported to hold the longest-lived lava lake known, present since at least 1906. [Wikipedia: Erta Ale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erta_Ale)

**Dallol** is a low salt volcano and hydrothermal field on the salt plain. Hot magma heats groundwater flowing in from the surrounding highlands. The water rises through the evaporites, dissolves salt and potash, and emerges as supersaturated brine in hot springs; as it evaporates it builds terraces coloured white, yellow, orange and green by sulphur, dissolved iron and algae. The most recent explosive event was a phreatic (steam-driven) eruption in 1926 that produced a small maar crater. [Geology.com](https://geology.com/stories/13/dallol)

## Heat and climate

Reported temperatures vary with the record used. Geology.com gives an average daily maximum of 106 °F (about 41 °C) and an annual mean of 94 °F (about 34.4 °C) for the Dallol area, and a VOA report that appears to date from around 2015 likewise gives an average of 34.4 °C and almost no rain. [Geology.com](https://geology.com/stories/13/dallol) [VOA: Ethiopia salt trade, Danakil Depression](https://www.voanews.com/a/ethiopia-salt-trade-danakil-depression-afar-mining-extraction-industry/1662972.html) These are long-term averages, not one-day extremes, and they are why the area is often called one of the hottest places on Earth. We have not located an official meteorological dataset to confirm exact station figures, so treat them as approximate.

## Life at the limit

A 2019 study in _Nature Ecology & Evolution_ sampled the Dallol–Danakil area along gradients of extreme acidity, salinity and temperature. The authors identified two physicochemical barriers to life where liquid water is present: very high chaotropicity and low water activity in magnesium- and calcium-rich brines, and the combination of hyperacidity (pH around 0) with salt saturation. Where life did occur it was dominated by diverse, ultrasmall archaea. They also found cells being silicified, and non-living structures that mimic cells, which they say calls for caution in interpreting biosignatures on Earth and beyond. [Belilla et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution (2019): Hyperdiverse archaea near life limits at the polyextreme geothermal Dallol area](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-019-1005-0)

## The salt trade

People live and work here. Afar salt miners cut slabs from the crust with hoes and axes and load them on camels. A VOA report, written around 2015, describes caravans that make a four-day trek between the salt flats and Berahile, where salt is sold on to merchants and then trucked across the country. Salt blocks were once used as money, and they are still sold widely, many to farmers as mineral supplements for livestock. [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/ethiopia-salt-trade-danakil-depression-afar-mining-extraction-industry/1662972.html)

The same report describes a pressure point: a paved road from Mekelle toward Dallol was being built, easing transport but raising fears among local clan leaders of mechanised industrial extraction. One clan leader quoted said "Everything has to be initiated from the community." Industrial salt production was already operating at Lake Afdera further south. [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/ethiopia-salt-trade-danakil-depression-afar-mining-extraction-industry/1662972.html) Conditions will have changed since that report, and this article does not claim to describe the trade today.

## A hazardous place to visit

Geology.com warns that Dallol's surface can be a thin salt crust over pools of hot acidic water, that toxic gases are sometimes released, and that visits have been risky because of the severe climate, the remote location and repeated attacks on tourists, with armed guards accompanying many groups. [Geology.com](https://geology.com/stories/13/dallol) Anyone planning a visit should consult current official travel advice.

## Closing

The Danakil is a place where a continent is slowly splitting and where people have long found a livelihood in the salt left behind by an ancient sea. Its geology and its microbes are active research subjects, and many of its numbers, from depth to temperature, are ranges rather than single settled values.

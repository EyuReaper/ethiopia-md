# Geography of Ethiopia

Ethiopia is located in the Horn of Africa, and at 1,127,127 square kilometers in area, it ranks among Africa's largest countries. [Wikipedia: Geography of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Ethiopia) Although it lies not far from the Red Sea, it has been a landlocked country since Eritrea declared its independence in Ginbot 1985 E.C.; even so, its mountainous highlands, deep valleys, and diverse climate zones make Ethiopia stand out for its geographical distinctiveness among East African countries.

## Highlands and High Mountains

Ethiopia's highlands are the largest continuous stretch of high-elevation terrain in Africa, with an average elevation of about 1,600 meters; their elevation reaches from 1,290 to 3,000 meters, and more than 25 mountains rise above 4,000 meters. Ras Dashen, found in the Simien Mountains, at 4,543 meters is not only Ethiopia's highest peak but also one of the highest mountains in Africa; Tullu Dimtu, in the Bale Mountains, ranks second at 4,377 meters. These highlands are the foundation of Ethiopia's agricultural productivity, its population distribution, and its climatic diversity.

## The Great Rift Valley, Rivers, and Lakes

The Great East African Rift Valley, which stretches from Mozambique to the Jordan Valley, cuts through Ethiopia's highlands into a northeastern and a southwestern portion; within Ethiopia's main rift valley, seven major lakes are arrayed from south to north — Chamo, Abaya, Awasa, Shala, Abijatta, Langano, and Ziway; the largest, Lake Abaya, covers about 1,160 square kilometers, and many of the lakes also serve as tourist attractions known for their fish resources and wildlife diversity. [Rift Valley Ethiopia Tours: The Rift Valley Lakes](https://riftvalleyethiopiatours.com/attractions/natural-attractions/35-rift-valley-lakes.html) Lake Tana, found in the western highlands, is Ethiopia's largest lake and is also the source of the Abay River (the Blue Nile); together with the Tekeze and Sobat rivers, it contributes nearly 85 percent of the Nile's water that reaches Egypt. By contrast, the Danakil Depression in the northeast lies 125 meters below sea level and is considered one of the hottest places on Earth.

## Climate Zones and Seasons

Elevation differences largely determine Ethiopia's climate; by traditional classification, the country is divided into three main climate zones: Kolla, the hot zone below 1,830 meters; Woyna Dega, the temperate zone from 1,830 to 2,440 meters; and Dega, the cool zone above 2,440 meters. The rainy seasons are similarly divided into three: Kiremt, which lasts from Sene to Meskerem and brings 50 to 80 percent of annual rainfall; Belg, which runs from Yekatit to Ginbot and brings light rain; and Bega, the dry season from Tikimt to Tir. This seasonal system is not only the foundation of Ethiopian agriculture but also sets the rhythm of life for millions of farmers.

## Waterfalls and the Southern River Valleys

Near Lake Tana lies the Tis Abay ("Smoke of the Nile") waterfall, one of Ethiopia's best-known natural attractions, where the Abay River plunges down forcefully after leaving Lake Tana. In southwestern Ethiopia lies the Omo River valley; the Lower Omo Valley, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1972 E.C., is not only a site where numerous early human fossils crucial to the study of human evolution have been found, but is also a distinctive cultural zone home to numerous ethnic groups such as the Mursi, Suri, Nyangatom, and Dizi. [Wikivoyage: Lower Valley of the Omo](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Lower_Valley_of_the_Omo)

## Tectonic Activity and Volcanoes

The Afar Triangle, located in the area of the Danakil Depression, is a unique geological site where the African, Arabian, and Somali tectonic plates meet; as these plates gradually drift apart, the land is slowly being torn apart. Erta Ale volcano, located within this same rift zone, is one of Ethiopia's active volcanoes, and has held a persistent lava lake, simmering at its summit continuously since at least 1959 E.C. (possibly since 1898 E.C.); this makes it one of the few volcanoes in the world to have sustained a lava lake for such a long period. [NASA Earth Observatory: The Smoking Mountain](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/152150/the-smoking-mountain)

## Landlocked Status and Neighboring Countries

Ethiopia shares borders with Eritrea, Djibouti, Somalia, Kenya, South Sudan, and Sudan, and has been landlocked since Eritrea declared its independence in Ginbot 1985 E.C.; before that, it used the ports of Massawa and Assab. Because relations were completely severed by the war fought between Ethiopia and Eritrea from 1990 to 1992 E.C., Ethiopia has become largely dependent on the port of Djibouti for its trade. [Volunteer Africa: Historical background for the landlocked status of Ethiopia](https://volunteerafrica.fi/ethiopia-landlock-status/) This geographic position has remained an ongoing issue affecting the country's economic and strategic direction.

## Population Distribution and Urban Development

Because Ethiopia's highlands offer fertile soil and a cool climate free of malaria-carrying mosquitoes, most of the country's population has, since ancient times, settled in these high-elevation areas; the lowlands, by contrast, have remained relatively sparsely populated. The capital, Addis Ababa, sits at the foot of Entoto Mountain at an elevation of 2,355 meters, making it one of the world's highest capital cities — after those of Bolivia, Ecuador, and Colombia — and the highest capital city in Africa. [WorldAtlas: The Highest Capital Cities in the World](https://www.worldatlas.com/cities/the-highest-capital-cities-in-the-world.html) Rapid urbanization is also increasingly placing additional pressure on the central highland area.

## Biodiversity and Protected Areas

Ethiopia's highlands are home to a wide variety of wildlife. Simien Mountains National Park, inscribed by UNESCO in 1970 E.C. as one of the country's first World Heritage Sites, hosts the Walia ibex, found nowhere else in the world, the extremely rare Ethiopian wolf, and gelada monkeys. [Wikipedia: Simien Mountains National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simien_Mountains_National_Park) Bale Mountains National Park, in the southeast, hosts close to 500 Ethiopian wolves, the largest population of the species, and is also home to species found only in that area, such as the mountain nyala and the giant mole-rat. This natural diversity has made Ethiopia one of East Africa's most important biodiversity conservation regions; more than eight of its natural and cultural heritage sites have been inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

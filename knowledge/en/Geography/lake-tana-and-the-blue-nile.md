# Lake Tana and the Blue Nile: Ethiopia's Largest Lake and Its Island Monasteries

**In 30 seconds.** Lake Tana (ጣና ሐይቅ) lies at about 1,800 m in the north-western highlands, near the city of Bahir Dar. It is the largest lake in Ethiopia, shallow, fed by a handful of large rivers, and drained by a single outlet, the Blue Nile, known in Ethiopia as the Abay (ዓባይ). Its islands and shores hold monasteries that date back to the 13th century, its wetlands shelter very large numbers of waterbirds, and most of its fish species occur nowhere else. An invasive weed and the pressures of a growing region are the main threats.

## Size and setting

Sources give somewhat different dimensions, because the lake rises and falls with the seasons. A freshwater ecoregion profile prepared by WWF and reviewed by a Wageningen University specialist puts the lake at about 3,150 km² within a basin of about 16,500 km², with a mean depth of 8 m, and notes that the average difference between the lowest level (May-June) and highest (September-October) is about 1.5 m. [Freshwater Ecoregions of the World: Lake Tana](https://feow.org/ecoregions/details/526) Wikipedia gives a surface of 3,000 to 3,500 km², a maximum depth of about 15 m, a length of about 84 km and an elevation of 1,788 m. [Wikipedia: Lake Tana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tana) A BirdLife and Key Biodiversity Areas factsheet gives similar order-of-magnitude figures, with a maximum depth of about 14 m. [Key Biodiversity Areas: Bahir Dar – Lake Tana](https://www.keybiodiversityareas.org/site/factsheet/6241)

The lake owes its existence to volcanism. The ecoregion profile explains that a volcanic blockage reversed the previously north-flowing river system, and Wikipedia dates the damming to the early Pleistocene, roughly five million years ago. [FEOW](https://feow.org/ecoregions/details/526) [Wikipedia: Lake Tana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tana) Four large perennial rivers feed it, the Gilgel Abay (also called the Little Abay), Ribb, Gumara and Megech, along with many seasonal streams. [FEOW](https://feow.org/ecoregions/details/526) Only the Blue Nile leaves the lake. [FEOW](https://feow.org/ecoregions/details/526)

## The source of the Blue Nile

Whether the lake or a spring is "the source" depends on definition. Ethiopia's UNESCO tentative-list entry for the Lake Tana island monasteries describes the lake and the Blue Nile as one of the long-sought mysteries of the ancient world, and the ecoregion profile calls Lake Tana "the source of the Blue Nile". [UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Lake Tana Island Monasteries and its Adjacent Wetland (Tentative List)](https://whc.unesco.org/fr/listesindicatives/6580) [FEOW](https://feow.org/ecoregions/details/526) Wikipedia's Blue Nile article, by contrast, lists the river's source as the Gish Abay spring at Sekela, south of the lake, and reports that the Spanish Jesuit Pedro Páez reached the river's source there on 21 April 1618. [Wikipedia: Blue Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Nile) Both statements can be true: the Gilgel Abay is the lake's largest inflow, and the Abay emerges from the lake at its southern end near Bahir Dar.

Downstream of the lake the river drops over the Tis Abay falls, whose name means "great smoke". The ecoregion profile gives the falls' height as about 40 m and notes that they effectively isolate the lake's freshwater fauna from the rest of the Nile. [FEOW](https://feow.org/ecoregions/details/526) Below them the Abay enters a canyon about 400 km long, and flows on to join the White Nile at Khartoum. [Wikipedia: Blue Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Nile) Water from this basin is central to regional debates about the Nile; see [the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam](/en/economy/grand-ethiopian-renaissance-dam) for that dispute, which is outside the scope of this article.

## Island monasteries

Lake Tana has long been a religious centre of Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity. The UNESCO tentative-list entry says the lake has 37 islands, around 27 of which have monasteries, churches or the ruins of palaces, and that these have protected Ethiopian cultural treasures since the 13th century. It also records a local tradition that the Ark of the Covenant was kept at Tana Qirqos for several centuries. [UNESCO tentative list](https://whc.unesco.org/fr/listesindicatives/6580) Island counts vary with lake level and with who counted: Wikipedia notes a 17th-century missionary counted 21 islands, with seven or eight bearing monasteries, and that a 20th-century geographer named 37 islands, 19 of which had or have monasteries or churches. [Wikipedia: Lake Tana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tana) The monasteries Ura Kidane Mehret, Narga Selassie and Kebran Gabriel are among those named in that account.

## Biodiversity

The ecoregion profile reports that about 70% of the lake's fish species are endemic, including eighteen endemic cyprinids; it counts fifteen large barbs of the subgenus _Labeobarbus_ among them, and says their taxonomy is under revision. [FEOW](https://feow.org/ecoregions/details/526) Wikipedia describes this group as one of only two known cyprinid species flocks, with different species specialised for different foods. [Wikipedia: Lake Tana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tana)

The lake and its wetlands are also a bird area. BirdLife's factsheet describes papyrus beds from which the local reed boats (_tankwa_) are made, and says waterbird numbers are thought to exceed 20,000 seasonally. [Key Biodiversity Areas](https://www.keybiodiversityareas.org/site/factsheet/6241) The factsheet is old, and the count date it quotes is December 1993, so it should be treated as indicative only.

## Protection

UNESCO lists Lake Tana as a biosphere reserve with 2015 as its year of nomination, in the Amhara region about 563 km north-west of Addis Ababa. [UNESCO MAB: Lake Tana](https://www.unesco.org/en/mab/lake-tana) The International Co-ordinating Council added the site to the World Network of Biosphere Reserves at its meeting in Paris on 8 to 12 June 2015 (announced 9 June), and UNESCO gives its total area as 695,885 hectares, calling it "a hotspot of biodiversity" and an Important Bird Area. [UNESCO: Twenty new sites added to the World Network of Biosphere Reserves](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/twenty-new-sites-added-unescos-world-network-biosphere-reserves)

## Threats: the water hyacinth

The invasive water hyacinth (_Eichhornia crassipes_, now often classed as _Pontederia crassipes_) was reported on Lake Tana from 2011. A 2024 study in _Frontiers in Environmental Science_ used Sentinel satellite images and a random-forest model to estimate cover in the wet and dry seasons. It found about 686.5 and 650.4 ha in 2016, 1,851 and 1,259 ha in 2018, 1,396.7 and 1,305.7 ha in 2020, and 1,436.5 and 1,216.5 ha in 2022. [Frontiers in Environmental Science (2024): Spatiotemporal pattern of water hyacinth (Pontederia crassipes) distribution in Lake Tana, Ethiopia, using a random forest machine learning model](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2024.1476014/full) The same paper reports that it first appeared in 2011 at 80 to 100 hectares near the Megech river mouth, and cites an earlier study that put the spread at about 20,000 ha within a year. These figures differ greatly because of different methods and definitions, and a reader should not treat any single number as settled. The lake level is also managed: Wikipedia notes that the outflow has been regulated by a control weir that feeds a hydropower station. [Wikipedia: Lake Tana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tana)

## Closing

Lake Tana is a water source, a sacred landscape and a site of research on fish evolution. Its numbers, from island counts to weed cover, depend on who measured and when, and this article has tried to say so wherever sources disagree.

# Endemic Wildlife of Ethiopia: Evolutionary Speciation on the High Plateau

The Ethiopian highlands rise well above the dry lowlands of the Horn of Africa, and their cold, wet plateaus are cut off from similar habitat elsewhere in Africa. Over long periods that isolation has produced animals and plants found nowhere else. Ethiopia's Fifth National Report to the Convention on Biological Diversity, prepared by the Ethiopian Biodiversity Institute in 2014, records 284 wild mammal species and 861 bird species in the country. Of these, 29 mammals, 18 birds, 10 reptiles, 40 fish, 25 amphibians and 7 arthropods are listed as endemic, along with about 10 percent of an estimated 6,000 species of higher plants. [Ethiopian Biodiversity Institute: Ethiopia's Fifth National Report to the CBD (2014)](https://www.cbd.int/doc/world/et/et-nr-05-en.pdf) Counts like these change as taxonomy is revised and surveys improve, so they are best read as dated estimates. The highlands form part of the Eastern Afromontane biodiversity hotspot. [UNESCO: Simien National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/)

## Why the Highlands Breed Endemics

Many of Ethiopia's best-known endemics live in afroalpine grassland and heath, above the tree line. Two protected areas hold much of this habitat. Simien in the north is part of the Afroalpine Centre of Plant Diversity. [UNESCO: Simien](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) Bale in the southeast contains the largest intact and contiguous expanse of afroalpine habitat in the world, where more than 80 percent of species are endemic. [UNESCO: Bale Mountains National Park](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/111/) The Rift Valley runs between these two highland blocks, so some species and subspecies occur only on one side of it. The Bale monkey, for example, is restricted to highlands east of the Rift. [UNESCO: Bale](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/111/)

## Flagship Mammals

### Ethiopian Wolf (_Canis simensis_, ቀይ ቀበሮ)

Despite its fox-like look, the Ethiopian wolf descends from a grey-wolf-like ancestor that came into Africa from Eurasia. It lives in close-knit family packs but hunts alone, mostly for afroalpine rodents. [Ethiopian Wolf Conservation Programme: About Ethiopian Wolves](https://www.ethiopianwolf.org/the-wolves/about-ethiopian-wolves) The programme estimates that about 500 survive in six small populations, with 453 wolves in its latest estimate. [Ethiopian Wolf Conservation Programme](https://www.ethiopianwolf.org/) It names habitat loss and diseases carried by domestic dogs as the most pressing threats. [Ethiopian Wolf Conservation Programme: About](https://www.ethiopianwolf.org/the-wolves/about-ethiopian-wolves) Bale holds the most important population. [UNESCO: Bale](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/111/) There the wolves rely heavily on the giant mole-rat (_Tachyoryctes macrocephalus_), whose entire global population lives in the Bale Mountains. [Farm Africa / BERSMP: The Significance of the Bale Mountains (2007)](https://farmafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Policy-Brief-1-BERSMP.pdf)

### Walia Ibex (_Capra walie_, ዋልያ)

The walia is a wild goat found only on the cliffs of the Simien Mountains, within a range of about 100 square kilometers. [Scholte et al.: Severe decline of the only remaining population of walia ibex (Oryx, 2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/severe-decline-of-the-only-remaining-population-of-walia-ibex-in-ethiopia-proposed-actions-and-recommended-recategorization-as-critically-endangered/93CD6532B724CCC686E82F434D367F32) Its record shows how fragile recovery can be. The national park was created to protect about 200 survivors. Censuses counted 865 animals in 2015, about 650 in 2019–2021 and 306 in 2024. Most local people interviewed blamed poaching, made worse by the instability of the Covid-19 period and the 2021–2022 war. [Scholte et al. (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/severe-decline-of-the-only-remaining-population-of-walia-ibex-in-ethiopia-proposed-actions-and-recommended-recategorization-as-critically-endangered/93CD6532B724CCC686E82F434D367F32) The IUCN status has moved several times: Endangered in 1986, Critically Endangered in 1996, Endangered in 2008, later Vulnerable, and Critically Endangered again in 2026. A count in December 2025 found only 271 animals. [Mongabay: Ethiopia's iconic Walia ibex is critically endangered once again](https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/2026/07/ethiopias-iconic-walia-ibex-is-critically-endangered-once-again/)

### Gelada (_Theropithecus gelada_, ጭላዳ)

Sometimes called the "bleeding-heart monkey" for the bare red patch on its chest, the gelada lives only in the Ethiopian highlands, at about 1,800 to 4,400 meters. It is the only living species of its genus and the only primate that is mainly a grazer, with grass making up to 90 percent of its diet. [Wikipedia: Gelada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelada) Geladas sleep on cliff faces and feed on the grasslands of the deep gorges of the central plateau, and in Simien they are one of the flagship species protected by the park. [Wikipedia: Gelada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelada) [UNESCO: Simien](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/)

### Mountain Nyala (_Tragelaphus buxtoni_, የደጋ አጋዘን)

The mountain nyala is a large spiral-horned antelope. Major Ivor Buxton brought a specimen to England in 1908, and the naturalist Richard Lydekker described the species in 1910, making it the last large antelope discovered in Africa. [Wikipedia: Mountain nyala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_nyala) About two-thirds of the global population lives in Bale Mountains National Park. [UNESCO: Bale](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/111/) Population estimates are uncertain. A 2012 habitat model of the Bale Mountains rated only about 17 percent of the study area as good or optimal habitat for the species, reflecting widespread habitat degradation caused by people. [Evangelista et al.: Assessing habitat quality of the mountain nyala in the Bale Mountains (Current Zoology, 2012)](https://doi.org/10.1093/czoolo/58.4.525)

## Small Mammals, Amphibians and Birds

The big four are only part of the picture. Many endemics are small and little studied. In Bale alone, UNESCO lists 23 endemic mammals, including eight rodents, as well as the Harenna shrew and several frogs and toads known only from those mountains. [UNESCO: Bale](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/111/) Amphibians are among Ethiopia's most endemic animal groups, with 25 endemic species out of 63 recorded. [Ethiopian Biodiversity Institute (2014)](https://www.cbd.int/doc/world/et/et-nr-05-en.pdf)

Bird endemism is often shared with neighboring Eritrea, which has similar highland habitat. Simien alone has 16 bird species endemic to Ethiopia and/or Eritrea. [UNESCO: Simien](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) Two birds found only in Ethiopia show the range:

- **Blue-winged goose (_Cyanochen cyanoptera_)**, a stocky highland waterbird and the only member of its genus. It is listed as Near Threatened. [Wikipedia: Blue-winged goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-winged_goose)
- **Ruspoli's turaco (_Menelikornis ruspolii_, formerly _Tauraco ruspolii_)**, which lives in the forests of southern Ethiopia and is threatened by habitat loss. It is also listed as Near Threatened. [Wikipedia: Ruspoli's turaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Ruspoli%27s_turaco)

## Conservation and Coexistence

Ethiopia's endemics share their habitat with farmers and herders. The government report names habitat conversion, unsustainable use of natural resources, invasive species, climate change and pollution as the main direct threats to Ethiopia's biodiversity, with population growth and poverty as indirect causes. [Ethiopian Biodiversity Institute (2014)](https://www.cbd.int/doc/world/et/et-nr-05-en.pdf) In Simien and Bale, UNESCO lists settlement, cultivation and livestock grazing inside the parks as the main pressures. [UNESCO: Simien](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/9/) [UNESCO: Bale](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/111/) The responses now in use include participatory grazing-management plans, buffer zones governed with neighboring _kebeles_, long-term monitoring of wolf populations, and community ambassadors against poaching. [UNESCO: Bale](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/111/) [Ethiopian Wolf Conservation Programme](https://www.ethiopianwolf.org/) [Scholte et al. (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/severe-decline-of-the-only-remaining-population-of-walia-ibex-in-ethiopia-proposed-actions-and-recommended-recategorization-as-critically-endangered/93CD6532B724CCC686E82F434D367F32) The walia's recent collapse after decades of recovery shows that these gains depend on security and funding as much as on biology.

For the two parks where most of these species live, see [the Bale Mountains](/en/nature/bale-mountains-ecosystem) and [the Simien Mountains](/en/geography/simien-mountains).

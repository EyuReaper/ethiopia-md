# Endemic Wildlife of Ethiopia: Evolutionary Speciation on the High Plateau

Elevated above the surrounding arid lowlands of the Horn of Africa, the Ethiopian plateau functions ecologically as an archipelago of sky-islands. Recognized by Conservation International as the core of the Eastern Afromontane Biodiversity Hotspot, Ethiopia harbors exceptional levels of evolutionary speciation and endemism: over 30 mammal species, more than 20 bird species, and thousands of plants are found nowhere else on the planet. [Kingdon: The Kingdon Field Guide to African Mammals](https://www.bloomsbury.com/uk/kingdon-field-guide-to-african-mammals-9781472912367/)

## The Flagship Mammalian Endemics

### 1. The Ethiopian Wolf (_Canis simensis_)

Africa’s most endangered carnivore and the rarest canid in the world. With fewer than 500 adult individuals surviving in the wild, its distribution is restricted exclusively to fragmented afroalpine pockets above 3,000 meters, with more than half the global population concentrated in the Bale Mountains. [IUCN: Canis simensis](https://www.iucnredlist.org/) Specialized in hunting high-altitude fossorial rodents like the giant root-rat, the wolf faces severe threats from domestic dog-borne rabies and canine distemper viruses.

### 2. The Walia Ibex (_Capra walie_)

A charismatic wild caprid endemic entirely to the precipitous northern escarpments of the Simien Mountains. Possessing majestic ridged horns curving over one meter long in mature males, the species was historically decimated by poaching during regional conflicts. Through dedicated community-scout monitoring and anti-poaching patrols by the Ethiopian Wildlife Conservation Authority (EWCA), populations have rebounded from fewer than 150 individuals in the 1960s to over 500 today. [EWCA](http://www.ewca.gov.et/)

### 3. The Gelada Baboon (_Theropithecus gelada_)

The sole surviving member of the once-widespread graminivorous primate genus _Theropithecus_. Colloquially known as the "bleeding-heart monkey" due to the triangular patch of bare scarlet skin on its chest, the Gelada is the world’s only strictly grass-eating monkey. Highly social and vocal, Geladas form multi-tiered bands numbering up to 400 individuals along the cliff rims of the central and northern highlands.

### 4. The Mountain Nyala (_Tragelaphus buxtoni_)

Discovered by British naturalist Major Ivor Buxton in 1908, the Mountain Nyala was the last large African antelope described by Western taxonomy. Exhibiting elegant spiral horns and vertical white side stripes, it inhabits the ericaceous heather zones and montane cedar forests of the Bale Mountains and Arsi massif.

## Avian and Floral Endemism

Ethiopia's avifauna comprises over 860 recorded species, with unique endemics including:

- **Prince Ruspoli's Turaco (_Tauraco ruspolii_)**: A magnificent crimson-crested, green-bodied frugivore restricted to southern juniper and acacia woodlands.
- **Blue-winged Goose (_Cyanochen cyanoptera_)**: An ancient highland waterfowl adapted to freezing alpine tarns.
- **Harwood's Francolin (_Pternistis harwoodi_)**: Endemic to the deep thornscrub gorges of the Blue Nile River basin.

## Conservation Strategies and Sustainable Coexistence

Protecting this fragile endemic heritage requires balancing biodiversity conservation with the agricultural livelihoods of expanding rural populations. The Ethiopian Wildlife Conservation Authority (EWCA), partnered with international organizations like the Frankfurt Zoological Society and the Born Free Foundation, has expanded community-managed buffer zones and sustainable high-altitude eco-tourism to incentivize local habitat stewardship.

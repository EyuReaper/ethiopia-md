# The Habesha Kemis and Shema Weaving: The Living Art of Ethiopian Dress

The **Habesha Kemis** is the timeless traditional ankle-length dress worn by Ethiopian women, embodying a rich heritage of artisanal craftsmanship, dignity, and cultural pride. [Spring & Hudson: North African Textiles](https://www.britishmuseum.org/) Handwoven from pure highland cotton and adorned with kaleidoscopic, geometrically embroidered borders (_tibeb_), this attire remains the quintessential garment of choice for major religious celebrations, weddings, and communal feasts across the country and the global diaspora.

## The Artisanal Cycle: From Raw Cotton to the Pit Loom

The creation of authentic Ethiopian textiles (_shema_) involves a specialized division of artisanal labor passed down through generations:

1. **The Hand-Spindle (_Inzerat_)**: Women card and clean raw, unbleached domestic cotton by hand, spinning it into ultra-fine thread (_fetil_) using a balanced wooden drop spindle weighted with clay or bone whorls.
2. **The Pit-Loom Weaver (_Shemane_)**: The warp and weft threads are mounted onto a traditional wooden pit loom (_irbabo_), dug directly into the earth. The weaver sits on the edge of the pit, rhythmically operating foot treadles and hand shuttles to weave light, breathable cotton cloth. Master weavers from the **Dorze** community in the southern Gamo highlands are nationally renowned as the foremost masters of this intricate weaving craft.
3. **The Embroidered Border (_Tibeb_)**: The cuffs, necklines, and lower hems are embellished with _tibeb_—dazzling geometric bands incorporating traditional diamond, cross, and chevron motifs woven with vibrant silk or metallic threads.

## Anatomy of the Ensemble: Netela, Gabi, and Kuta

A traditional Ethiopian outfit is defined by the layering of matching woven garments:

- **Netela**: A delicate, semi-translucent double-layered cotton shawl bordered by a decorative tibeb band matching the dress. The draping of the netela conveys nuanced social and emotional etiquette:
  - Draped symmetrically over both shoulders: general decorum and formal church attendance.
  - Cross-draped across one shoulder with the right arm bare: joy, celebration, and active dancing.
  - Inverted with the embroidered border tucked inside: solemn bereavement, mourning, and fasting.
- **Gabi**: A thicker, four-layered warm cotton blanket worn draped around the shoulders by men and women alike against highland evening chills.
- **Kuta**: A lighter, elegant draped cotton cloak worn by men over trousers and a tunic for formal gatherings.

## Contemporary Haute Couture Renaissance

In recent years, the Habesha Kemis has transitioned from traditional wear to the global high-fashion runway. Contemporary Ethiopian fashion designers (such as supermodel Liya Kebede with her sustainable artisanal label Lemlem) have popularized handspun shema cloth internationally. Modern interpretations combine traditional pit-loom textures with contemporary silhouettes, evening gowns, and ready-to-wear luxury lines while sustaining rural artisan weaving cooperatives across Ethiopia.

# The Habesha Kemis and Shema Weaving: The Living Art of Ethiopian Dress

The _habesha kemis_ (የሐበሻ ቀሚስ) is the traditional handwoven cotton dress of the highland peoples of Ethiopia, including the Amhara and Tigrayans. The white, ankle-length dress has been widely adopted as a national symbol. [Wikipedia: Clothing in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing_in_Ethiopia) It is made from _shema_ (ሸማ), the light handwoven cotton cloth of the northern and central highlands, and is worn with matching shawls such as the _netela_. [Wikipedia: Clothing in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing_in_Ethiopia) Its colored border, the _tibeb_ (ጥበብ), is woven into the cloth with a supplementary weft. As a multicolored band, the textile scholar Jannes Waples Gibson notes, it is a relatively recent addition, dating from the era of Emperor Menelik II. [Gibson, Textile Society of America (1998)](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/168) In 2025 a weaver in Addis Ababa told AFP that the simplest dress takes a week to weave and an elaborately bordered one twice as long. [eNCA/AFP: Ethiopia's weavers struggle to keep garment tradition alive](https://www.enca.com/lifestyle/ethiopias-weavers-struggle-keep-garment-tradition-alive)

## From Cotton to Cloth

Ethiopia has a long history of cotton weaving. The Amhara provinces of Gondar, Wollo, Gojjam and Shewa have historically been its center, and in 2021 the Amhara Region accounted for 76% of the country's small-scale cotton farmers. [Wikipedia: Clothing in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing_in_Ethiopia) The Japanese researcher Jumpei Itagaki studied weaving in and around Bahir Dar in 2008 to 2010. He reports that the cotton used there is mainly grown in Welo and bought in the market. An indigenous tree cotton, _Gossypium arboreum_, was used in the past but no longer is. [Itagaki, African Study Monographs (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content)

The work is divided by gender. Among the Amhara, Itagaki found, men do the weaving, while women beat the raw cotton and spin the weft by hand on a spindle called an _inzirt_ (እንዝርት). [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content) Full-time weavers use a pit treadle loom, set over a hole 70 to 80 cm deep at the weaver's feet. Migrant weavers and the many prisoners who weave in Bahir Dar more often use a loom mounted on a legless chair. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content)

Cloth is measured in _kind_ (ክንድ), the distance from fingertip to elbow, about 50 cm. A netela takes 12 kind, roughly 6 meters, which is cut into two lengths and sewn together. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content) The weaving is plain weave, the simplest structure, but Itagaki notes that it needs constant care to come out even. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content)

## Who Wove Ethiopia's Cloth

Weaving has passed between communities over the centuries. Itagaki, citing the historian Kaplan (1992), notes that in the early 17th century Jews were almost the only cotton weavers, and that a century later they had been replaced, at least in Gondar, by Muslims. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content)

From the 19th century the Dorze, a people of the southern highlands, became prominent weavers. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content) Dorze weavers settled in the Shiro Meda area of Addis Ababa, and by the early 1900s they had a reputation as the most skilled weavers of tibeb, though never the only ones. [Gibson (1998)](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/168) Before that, Gibson writes, the Dorze were known for heavier cloths such as the _bulluko_. Shiro Meda remains a major clothing market today. [Gibson (1998)](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/168) [Wikipedia: Clothing in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing_in_Ethiopia)

## Tibeb: The Woven Border

Gibson defines tibeb as the decoration or pattern handwoven with supplementary weft into the border of the shema, worn by both women and men. A border embroidered after weaving, rather than woven in, is called _tilf tibeb_. [Gibson (1998)](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/168) Before multicolored tibeb, members of the aristocracy wore shema with a single-color red border, the _tilet_, which Gibson suggests may be its precursor. [Gibson (1998)](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/168) Itagaki records simpler border patterns in Bahir Dar as well: stripes (_tilet_) or plain geometric bands (_gebgab_), between 2 and 30 cm wide. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content)

Tibeb also appears on the netela, the _kuta_ and the _gabi_, and in the vestments of Orthodox priests. The complete women's outfit of a kemis and a netela with matching tibeb is called _shema komblete_. [Gibson (1998)](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/168)

## Netela, Kuta and Gabi

The **netela** (ነጠላ) is the shawl worn with the kemis. Its name means "single" or "thin", a reference to its transparency. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content) How it is draped depends on the occasion and on the wearer's religion. Orthodox Christians wear it at church, on holidays, at weddings and funerals, and every day. For church it is spread lengthwise and wrapped around the body. For an Orthodox funeral it is folded in half, with the patterned end fixed over the head. Muslim women in the area wear the netela only at weddings and funerals, and drape it differently. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content) At Genna, Ethiopian Christmas, the white cotton netela is the customary dress. [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gena)

The **kuta** (ኩታ) uses about 24 kind (some 12 meters) of cloth, twice as much as the netela. Orthodox men wear it as formal dress for church and funerals. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content) The **gabi** (ጋቢ) uses about 50 kind (some 25 meters), cut into 3-meter lengths, stacked and sewn together, which makes it thick and warm. Among the Amhara it is worn regardless of religion. Men wear it at funerals, at church and every day, women wear it as winter clothing at home, and it is given to the groom at weddings. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content) Itagaki also notes that people often use "kuta" and "gabi" interchangeably. [Itagaki (2013)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/server/api/core/bitstreams/206bca05-8ec8-4ee0-8a79-34877a71d367/content)

## One Tradition Among Many

The shema tradition belongs mainly to the Christian highlands; Gibson notes that the shema was traditionally worn by the Christian populations of the northern and central highlands. [Gibson (1998)](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/168) Other Ethiopian peoples have their own dress. Leather and cowhide have long been essential materials among the Oromo and several pastoralist peoples of the south. Harari women traditionally wear distinctive embroidered dresses with silver jewelry. [Wikipedia: Clothing in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing_in_Ethiopia) Calling the habesha kemis "Ethiopian dress" is common, but it describes one regional tradition, not the whole country.

## Weavers, Designers and the Market Today

Handweaving is under economic pressure. In 2025, AFP visited an Addis Ababa workshop where a weaver with three decades of experience earned 10,000 to 15,000 birr (about USD 68 to 102) a month before the cost of materials, working nine hours a day, six days a week. [eNCA/AFP](https://www.enca.com/lifestyle/ethiopias-weavers-struggle-keep-garment-tradition-alive) Cheaper machine-made habesha kemis imported from China had undercut local weavers years earlier. Government restrictions came too late for many of them, and with inflation around 21.5% that year, according to the IMF, demand had slowed sharply. "There's no future," the workshop's youngest weaver, aged 23, told AFP. [eNCA/AFP](https://www.enca.com/lifestyle/ethiopias-weavers-struggle-keep-garment-tradition-alive)

Designers have tried to open new markets. The model Liya Kebede launched the label Lemlem ("to bloom" in Amharic) in 2007, with hand-spun, woven and embroidered clothing, to help preserve traditional weaving and give artisans work. [Wikipedia: Liya Kebede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liya_Kebede) "Weaving is an old tradition in Ethiopia and a central part of our cultural heritage," she told Soho House. "I wanted to play a part in keeping that craft alive and creating sustainable jobs for the artisans." [Soho House: How I launched Lemlem](https://www.sohohouse.com/house-notes/issue-005/work/how-i-launched-lemlem-liya-kebede) In Addis Ababa, the designer Mahlet Afework told The Reporter that when she started some 15 years earlier, traditional clothing was rarely worn every day. She has since built a brand around wearable designs drawn from tradition, though she says the domestic fashion industry is still held back by limited training and resources. [The Reporter: Stitching Tradition to Trend](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/41415/)

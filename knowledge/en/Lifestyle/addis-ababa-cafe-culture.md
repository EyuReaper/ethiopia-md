# Addis Ababa Café Culture and the Macchiato Heritage

Ethiopia is the home of the coffee ceremony, in which coffee is roasted, ground and brewed by hand in a clay _jebena_. Addis Ababa has also built a second coffee culture, with espresso machines, standing bars and the city's signature drink, the _macchiato_. Addis Fortune describes the macchiato, "a bold espresso softened with steamed milk", as one of the most enduring legacies of the Italian occupation era. For decades it has been the center of urban coffee habits. [Addis Fortune: Addis Abeba pours a new story in every cup](https://addisfortune.news/addis-abeba-pours-a-new-story-in-every-cup-where-tradition-meets-global-influence) The city's best-known café, Tomoca, was founded in 1953 and is modeled on Italian espresso bars. [The Guardian: 'It's rude not to offer three cups'](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) Today these cafés sit alongside street-side jebena stalls and a new wave of specialty coffee shops.

## Italian Roots

The espresso bar arrived with colonialism. Italy occupied Ethiopia from 1936 to 1941 and ruled it as part of Italian East Africa, which Mussolini proclaimed on June 1, 1936, after the Second Italo-Ethiopian War. [Wikipedia: Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_East_Africa) The scholar Diana Garvin of the University of Oregon has studied coffee culture under Fascism, tracing the bean from plantation to espresso machine. She argues that cafés became "key sites for promoting the Fascist imperial projects in East Africa", and that their architectural and artistic legacy persists today. [Garvin, Modern Italy (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1017/mit.2021.26)

The Italian occupation was a short episode in a much older story. Ethiopians drank coffee long before 1936. UNESCO notes that coffee drinking spread among Ethiopian Christians from the reign of Emperor Menelik II (1889 to 1913), who drank it openly. Today the domestic market consumes about 40% of the country's production. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee) What the Italian period added was a new way of serving coffee: by machine, in public, and to people on their way somewhere.

## Tomoca and the Piassa Café

The name TO.MO.CA comes from the Italian _Torrefazione Moderna Café_, "modern coffee roasting". [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/addis-abeba-pours-a-new-story-in-every-cup-where-tradition-meets-global-influence) The business was founded in 1953 by an Italian family and later acquired by Zewdu Meshesha, under whom it became one of the country's best-known coffee names. [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/addis-abeba-pours-a-new-story-in-every-cup-where-tradition-meets-global-influence) Quartz reports that Zewdu bought the roastery in 1953 itself and kept the Italian acronym. According to the company, it was the first time coffee was roasted commercially outside the home, and to win customers the family at first gave coffee away. [Quartz: Ethiopia's coffee ritual is being exported to China, Japan](https://qz.com/africa/1560013/ethiopias-coffee-ritual-is-being-exported-to-china-japan)

The flagship café stands in Piassa, a historic district whose Greek, Armenian and Italian buildings were among the first signs of the capital's internationalization. Its interior "feels mid-century European", and the menu still uses Italian names. [Quartz](https://qz.com/africa/1560013/ethiopias-coffee-ritual-is-being-exported-to-china-japan) As in an Italian bar, customers stand: Tomoca's cafés have high tables and no seats. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) A Vice reporter found one of its baristas pulling 30 to 40 kilograms of coffee a day through two old machines. [Vice: Jebena Buna Is the Only Way to Drink Ethiopian Coffee](https://www.vice.com/en/article/jebena-buna-is-the-only-way-to-drink-ethiopian-coffee)

Counts of Tomoca's branches vary with the date and the source. Quartz counted six outlets in Addis Ababa in 2019. [Quartz](https://qz.com/africa/1560013/ethiopias-coffee-ritual-is-being-exported-to-china-japan) The Guardian reported more than 20 branches in 2024, calling it "the closest thing Ethiopia has to Starbucks". [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) Addis Fortune put the figure at more than 21 branches in 2026. [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/addis-abeba-pours-a-new-story-in-every-cup-where-tradition-meets-global-influence) The company has also gone abroad, opening a branch in Tokyo in 2015 and in Nairobi in 2020. [Wikipedia: Tomoca Coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoca_Coffee)

## The Macchiato

In Addis Ababa the macchiato is so common that the Vice reporter called it almost a second national drink after jebena coffee. It is served small and concentrated, with the espresso shot poured through frothed milk, and Tomoca's baristas ask whether customers want it "normal" or "strong". [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/jebena-buna-is-the-only-way-to-drink-ethiopian-coffee) Taste runs dark. Tomoca's dark roast is the traditional default, and the company also sells medium and light roasts. [Wikipedia: Tomoca Coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoca_Coffee) A Tomoca production officer told Quartz that lighter, more acidic roasts are not popular with Ethiopians. "We don't like our coffee light," he said. "We eat spicy food so we like it strong." [Quartz](https://qz.com/africa/1560013/ethiopias-coffee-ritual-is-being-exported-to-china-japan)

Regulars treat the café as part of their daily routine. Addis Fortune profiled a 38-year-old banker who has stopped at the same Tomoca branch every weekday morning for six years for a dark macchiato. "I don't think I could drink any other coffee," she said. [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/addis-abeba-pours-a-new-story-in-every-cup-where-tradition-meets-global-influence)

## Street Coffee and the Busy City

The espresso bar did not replace the jebena; the two exist side by side. Women brewing coffee in jebenas on charcoal stoves are a fixture on almost every street corner in Ethiopia's cities. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) Many set up makeshift stalls outside restaurants and small businesses, roasting small batches of beans on the spot. Quartz describes these stalls as an easy way for women to earn money in the informal economy, a livelihood it warns could be threatened as the sector becomes more formal. [Quartz](https://qz.com/africa/1560013/ethiopias-coffee-ritual-is-being-exported-to-china-japan)

City life is one reason cafés have grown. The full home ceremony takes about an hour, and western-style coffee shops serving milky macchiatos have sprung up for a fast-growing professional class with no time to make traditional coffee at home. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) An accountant interviewed at a downtown Tomoca grew up in rural Gurage, where his family shared coffee-making with seven other households. He told the Guardian that he now drinks coffee mostly outside the home and saves the traditional ceremony for weekends. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) Local chains have followed. Kaldi's, named after the legendary goatherd, serves burgers, fries and macchiatos to young middle-class customers. [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/jebena-buna-is-the-only-way-to-drink-ethiopian-coffee)

## The Specialty Wave

The newest change is in what goes into the cup. In 2026 Addis Fortune reported that specialty cafés are spreading across the capital, with minimalist interiors, pour-over methods such as the V60, and light roasts that bring out the fruit, floral and citrus notes Ethiopian beans are known for abroad. [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/addis-abeba-pours-a-new-story-in-every-cup-where-tradition-meets-global-influence) Prices reflect the shift: at one of these cafés, an espresso cost 130 birr. The newspaper presents this culture as a sharp contrast to the macchiato traditions that shaped city habits for decades. [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/addis-abeba-pours-a-new-story-in-every-cup-where-tradition-meets-global-influence)

The atmosphere remains local, however. A young customer quoted by the paper said that while in Japan "you drink your pour-over in near silence", in Addis Ababa people in even the most minimalist café "are arguing about business, sharing laughs, and pulling chairs together". [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/addis-abeba-pours-a-new-story-in-every-cup-where-tradition-meets-global-influence)

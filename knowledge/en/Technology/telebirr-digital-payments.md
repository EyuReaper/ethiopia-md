# Telebirr and the Digital Payments Revolution

For generations, physical cash was the main medium of Ethiopia's economy; most citizens conducted their daily transactions in hand-to-hand cash without ever holding a bank account. When telebirr, Ethio Telecom's mobile money service, launched in Ginbot 2013 E.C. (May 11, 2021), that reality began to change quickly. In a short period telebirr attracted more than 54 million users and processed transactions worth more than two trillion birr, bringing millions of Ethiopians into the digital financial system. This article examines the platform's founding and growth, its relationship with banks and other payment platforms, and the challenges that remain.

## The Birth of telebirr

When telebirr launched in Ginbot 2013 E.C. (May 11, 2021), Ethiopia's financial system was extremely thin. Only nineteen commercial banks served a population approaching 115 million, and especially in rural areas millions of citizens were considered effectively unbanked ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/ethiopias-state-telecoms-monopoly-launches-mobile-money-service-idUSL1N2MY209/)). Ethio Telecom launched the service just one week after receiving its operational license from the National Bank of Ethiopia; Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed said at the inauguration ceremony that mobile financial services would be opened to competitors within a year.

The telebirr platform was built by the Chinese technology company Huawei, whose experts traveled to Ethiopia and completed the project even during the COVID-19 pandemic ([China Daily](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202105/13/WS609d1509a31024ad0babdbc4.html)). At the launch, Ethio Telecom CEO Frehiwot Tamru said the company aimed to reach 21 million users in the first year and 33 million within five years, with the longer-term hope that 40 to 50 percent of economic output would eventually be transacted through the platform. At launch, telebirr offered services including sending and receiving money, depositing and withdrawing cash through a nationwide agent network, paying electricity and water bills, buying mobile airtime, and receiving international remittances.

## From Zero to Fifty Million

telebirr outran the targets set for it. At the close of the first half of fiscal year 2017 E.C., in Tahsas 2017 E.C. (December 2024), Ethio Telecom's semi-annual performance report showed 51.5 million telebirr customers; around 1.03 trillion birr moved through the platform in those six months alone, bringing cumulative transactions since launch to 3.58 trillion birr ([Ethio telecom](https://www.ethiotelecom.et/ethio-telecom-2024-25-semi-annual-business-performance-report/)). In Yekatit 2017 E.C. (February 2025), the platform passed the 50-million-user milestone ([Ethiopian Monitor](https://ethiopianmonitor.com/2025/02/13/telebirr-subscribers-pass-50-million-mark)).

By the end of fiscal year 2017 E.C. (June 2025), telebirr had more than 54 million users, and 2.4 trillion birr was processed during that fiscal year alone. Its merchant network reached about 310,000 and its agent network about 320,000, while more than 3.91 million digital savers held a combined 24.59 billion birr ([Techpression](https://techpression.com/ethio-telecoms-telebirr-surpasses-54-million-users-processes-2-4-trillion-etb-in-transactions)). Working in partnership with Dashen Bank and the Commercial Bank of Ethiopia, telebirr has also extended microloans and savings products; and according to GSMA, the global mobile industry association, if enabling conditions are in place mobile money could add 5.3 billion dollars to Ethiopia's GDP and lift as many as 700,000 people out of poverty ([Ethiopian Monitor](https://ethiopianmonitor.com/2025/02/13/telebirr-subscribers-pass-50-million-mark)).

## Banks, Interoperability, and New Competitors

Because telebirr is operated by a telecom company rather than a bank, its relationship with the banking sector combines partnership with rivalry. EthSwitch, the national switch owned jointly by commercial banks and the National Bank of Ethiopia, makes bank cards and accounts interoperable across ATMs, point-of-sale terminals, and person-to-person transfers. Yet by the end of 2017 E.C., telebirr had not yet been integrated onto this national switch; observers also point to the largely rural population, low phone ownership and internet penetration, and persistent gender gaps in financial access as major constraints ([Shega](https://digitalfinance.shega.co/insights/articles/reimagining-interoperability-for-inclusive-payment-systems)).

At the same time, the National Bank of Ethiopia began licensing private payment instrument issuers: ArifPay received its license in Hidar 2014 E.C., Chapa Pay in Ginbot 2014 E.C., and SantimPay in Sene 2014 E.C. ([National Bank of Ethiopia](https://nbe.gov.et/payment-instrument-issuers-system-operators/)). This policy direction matched the promise made at telebirr's inauguration. Competition has also arrived from elsewhere on the continent: Safaricom Ethiopia launched M-PESA in Nehase 2015 E.C. (August 16, 2023). The Kenyan platform, which has operated since 2007, entered what Safaricom's interim CEO Stanley Njoroge described as a game-changer for financial inclusion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/safaricom-launches-m-pesa-mobile-money-service-ethiopia-2023-08-16/)).

## Remaining Challenges

The growth has not come without challenges. Interoperability between mobile wallets and the banking system is still maturing, and the central bank faces the continuing task of balancing innovation against financial stability. The 5.3-billion-dollar opportunity projected by GSMA depends precisely on these enablers — interoperability, infrastructure, and regulation ([Shega](https://digitalfinance.shega.co/insights/articles/reimagining-interoperability-for-inclusive-payment-systems); [Ethiopian Monitor](https://ethiopianmonitor.com/2025/02/13/telebirr-subscribers-pass-50-million-mark)). In other words, the success of Ethiopia's payments revolution will ultimately be measured not by any single company but by whether the whole foundation of the digital economy keeps expanding.

The story of telebirr is woven into Ethiopia's broader technology transformation. Fintech founders in the [[startup-ecosystem]] are building new services on top of telebirr and other payment rails, while the next wave of innovation is expected to follow the direction traced by [[ai-development]].

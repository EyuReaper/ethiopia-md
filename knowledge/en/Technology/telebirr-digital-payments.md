# Telebirr and the Digital Payments Revolution

For generations most Ethiopians paid for things in cash, and most adults had no bank account. In 2017, according to the World Bank's Global Findex survey, only 35 percent of Ethiopian adults had an account at a financial institution ([World Bank Blogs: Financial inclusion in Ethiopia, 10 takeaways from the latest Findex](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/africacan/financial-inclusion-in-ethiopia-10-takeaways-from-findex-2017)). Ethio Telecom's mobile money service, telebirr (ቴሌብር), launched on May 11, 2021 (Ginbot 3, 2013 E.C.), has changed that picture quickly. In the 2025/26 fiscal year it reached 60.6 million users and handled 4.19 trillion birr in transactions, and its cumulative transaction value since launch passed 9.13 trillion birr ([The Reporter: Data Overtakes Voice as Top Earner for Ethio Telecom](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/52133/)). This article covers how telebirr began, how it grew, how it fits with banks and new competitors, and what still limits it.

## The Launch in 2021

When telebirr launched, Ethiopia had 19 commercial banks for a population of about 115 million, and the banking system was widely seen as inefficient ([Reuters via Daily Dispatch: Ethiopia's state telecoms monopoly launches mobile money service](https://www.dailydispatch.co.za/africa/2021-05-12-ethiopias-state-telecoms-monopoly-launches-mobile-money-service/)). The National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) had issued Ethio Telecom the country's first payment instrument issuer license, number NPS PII/01/2021, on April 23, 2021, less than three weeks before launch ([National Bank of Ethiopia: Payment Instrument Issuers and System Operators](https://nbe.gov.et/payment-instrument-issuers-system-operators/)). At the launch, Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed said mobile financial services would be "opened up to competition after a year," and Ethio Telecom chief executive Frehiwot Tamiru set a target of 21 million users in the first year and 33 million within five years ([Reuters via Daily Dispatch](https://www.dailydispatch.co.za/africa/2021-05-12-ethiopias-state-telecoms-monopoly-launches-mobile-money-service/)).

The platform was built by the Chinese company Huawei. Its Ethiopia chief executive, Chen Mingliang, said the work took five months instead of the two years first projected. Frehiwot told China Daily she expected 40 to 50 percent of Ethiopia's economic activity to run through telebirr within five years ([China Daily: Ethiopia inaugurates Chinese-backed 'Telebirr' mobile money service](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202105/13/WS609d1509a31024ad0babdbc4.html)). From the start, customers could send, store and receive money using only a phone number.

## From Zero to Sixty Million

Telebirr passed its early targets well ahead of schedule. In the first half of the 2024/25 fiscal year (July to December 2024) it added 5 million users to reach 51.5 million, according to Ethio Telecom's semi-annual report as summarized by Ethiopian Monitor ([Ethiopian Monitor: Telebirr Subscribers Pass 50 Million Mark](https://ethiopianmonitor.com/2025/02/13/telebirr-subscribers-pass-50-million-mark)). By the end of that fiscal year it had 54.84 million users and had processed more than 2.38 trillion birr in the year. Its network had grown to 310,000 merchants and 320,000 agents, and through partner banks it had facilitated 11.24 billion birr in digital savings and disbursed 13.22 billion birr in micro-loans to 6.88 million customers ([TechAfrica News: Ethio Telecom achieves 83.2M subscribers](https://techafricanews.com/2025/07/25/ethio-telecom-achieves-83-2m-subscribers-and-73-revenue-surge-as-lead-strategy-concludes/)).

The 2025/26 results, covering the year to July 2026, show the pace continuing. Telebirr added 8.54 million users to reach 60.6 million. It handled 2.61 billion transactions, 145.3 percent more than the year before, worth 4.19 trillion birr. It provided 19.51 billion birr in collateral-free loans to 5.65 million customers and held 18.75 billion birr in digital savings from 1.3 million users. Even so, telebirr brought in only 3.6 percent of Ethio Telecom's revenue, compared with 31.1 percent from data and internet services ([The Reporter](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/52133/)). For the company, telebirr is a strategic platform more than a profit center.

Telebirr's savings and loans are offered in partnership with banks, including Dashen Bank and the Commercial Bank of Ethiopia ([Ethiopian Monitor](https://ethiopianmonitor.com/2025/02/13/telebirr-subscribers-pass-50-million-mark)). A GSMA report cited by Ethiopian Monitor estimates that mobile money could add 5.3 billion US dollars to Ethiopia's GDP and lift 700,000 people out of poverty if "certain enablers are in place."

## Banks, Interoperability and New Competitors

Because telebirr is run by a telecom operator, not a bank, its relationship with the banking sector mixes partnership and rivalry. Ethiopia's national switch, EthSwitch, is owned by financial institutions with the NBE in its ownership structure, and lets bank cards and accounts work across ATMs, point-of-sale terminals and person-to-person transfers. As of December 2024, Shega reported that telebirr was not included in EthSwitch's person-to-person figures. The same analysis lists other barriers: roughly 80 percent of Ethiopians live in rural areas, phone ownership is low, and financial access is unequal between men and women. It also notes that about 47 percent of ATMs and 77 percent of POS machines are in Addis Ababa ([Shega: Reimagining Interoperability for Inclusive Payment Systems](https://digitalfinance.shega.co/insights/articles/reimagining-interoperability-for-inclusive-payment-systems)).

The NBE has gradually opened the market, as Abiy promised. It licensed private payment system operators, including ArifPay (November 22, 2021), Chapa (May 20, 2022) and SantimPay (July 5, 2022), which run payment gateways for merchants. It has also licensed other wallet issuers: Kacha Digital Financial Services (June 2022), Safaricom's M-PESA (May 10, 2023) and Yaya (October 2023) ([National Bank of Ethiopia](https://nbe.gov.et/payment-instrument-issuers-system-operators/)).

The most direct competitor is M-PESA. Safaricom Ethiopia launched it on August 16, 2023, after a three-month pilot ([Safaricom Ethiopia: Safaricom Ethiopia goes live with M-PESA](https://www.safaricom.et/whats-new/latest/news-and-blogs/safaricom-ethiopia-goes-live-with-m-pesa)). At the time Safaricom had more than 2 million active users on its year-old voice and data network ([Reuters via The Star: Safaricom launches M-Pesa mobile money service in Ethiopia](https://thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2023/08/16/safaricom-launches-m-pesa-mobile-money-service-in-ethiopia)). Telebirr had a two-year head start.

## What Still Limits Telebirr

Telebirr's growth has outrun the infrastructure around it. Mobile wallets and bank systems are only partly interoperable, most ATMs and card terminals are concentrated in the capital, and rural phone ownership is low ([Shega](https://digitalfinance.shega.co/insights/articles/reimagining-interoperability-for-inclusive-payment-systems)). The NBE has to balance encouraging innovation against protecting financial stability, and the GSMA's 5.3-billion-dollar estimate depends on exactly those enablers ([Ethiopian Monitor](https://ethiopianmonitor.com/2025/02/13/telebirr-subscribers-pass-50-million-mark)). User counts also measure registrations, not active daily use. The figures above come from Ethio Telecom's own reporting as carried by the press, and no independent audit of active users was found. Whether Ethiopia's payments shift succeeds depends less on any single company than on whether that wider base keeps expanding.

Mobile money is also reaching community finance: fintech firms and banks are building digital versions of the iqub, Ethiopia's rotating savings group (see [Iddir and Iqub](/en/society/iddir-and-iqub-mutual-aid)). In Ethiopia's startup ecosystem, fintech founders are building services on top of telebirr and other payment rails. The country's next technology push is described in [Artificial Intelligence Development in Ethiopia](/en/technology/ai-development).

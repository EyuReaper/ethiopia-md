# Tej and Tella: Ethiopia's Honey Wine and Home-Brewed Beer

## 30-second overview

Tej (ጠጅ) is a honey wine flavored with gesho (_Rhamnus prinoides_), a bitter shrub often called "Ethiopian hops." Tella (ጠላ) is a household beer made from barley malt, other cereals and gesho. Both ferment spontaneously, without added starter cultures, and both are mostly made at home. Researchers describe them as among the most popular traditional alcoholic drinks in the country. [Mulaw et al., Frontiers in Nutrition 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841419/) They appear at weddings, naming ceremonies, festivals and funerals, and they are sold in small neighborhood bars. [Lemi, International Journal of Microbiology 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7042527)

## Tej: honey, water and gesho

A 2020 review in the journal _Foods_ describes tej as "an Ethiopian wine that uses honey as a substrate." The basic method is to mix honey and water at about 1 to 3 and ferment for two to three days. Gesho leaves and stems are then boiled, cooled and added, and the mixture ferments for a further 8 to 10 days in the hot season or about 20 days in the cold season. [Fentie et al., Foods 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/) Other sources give slightly different timings: a 2020 microbiology review gives about five days in warm weather and 15 to 20 days in cool conditions, and a 2025 review reports secondary fermentation of 8 to 21 days. [Lemi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7042527); [Mulaw et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841419/) The variation is a reminder that tej is a craft, not a standardized product.

The same reviews note the details that give each household its own version. The fermenting pot may be seasoned by smoking it over burning _Olea africana_ wood and gesho, and some producers add bark, roots, or malt powder. [Lemi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7042527); [Mulaw et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841419/) Wikipedia adds that producers often keep their exact additions secret and that cheaper versions replace some honey with sugar. [Wikipedia: Tej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej)

Good tej, according to the _Foods_ review, is yellow, sweet, fizzy and cloudy. [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/) Strength is where sources disagree most. The 2020 _Foods_ review reports total alcohol of 2.7 to 21.7 percent across samples, the 2025 review gives 7 to 11 percent by volume, and a travel essay in Roads & Kingdoms reports mild, medium and strong versions, the strongest reaching about 16 percent. [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/); [Mulaw et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841419/); [Roads & Kingdoms, 2016](https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2016/everymans-drink-descended-ancient-royal-libations/) A fair summary is that most tej sits in the 7 to 11 percent range, with strong outliers.

## Why honey matters

Tej is closely tied to Ethiopia's beekeeping. The _Foods_ review says Ethiopia could produce about 500,000 tons of honey a year, though actual production has not exceeded 10 percent of that potential, and that about 80 percent of the honey produced goes into tej. [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/) The 2025 review repeats the 80 percent figure, and Wikipedia says the same, so the number is widely quoted; it should be read as an estimate, not a measured statistic. [Mulaw et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841419/); [Wikipedia: Tej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej) Crude honey, with the wax and pollen of traditional hives, is preferred for quality tej. [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/)

## What is happening in the pot

No one adds yeast. Fermentation depends on lactic acid bacteria and yeasts already present in the ingredients, vessels and equipment, and their by-products add acidity, flavor and aroma. The yeasts reported include _Saccharomyces cerevisiae_, _Kluyveromyces bulgaricus_, _Debaryomyces phaffii_ and _Kluyveromyces veronae_, alongside _Lactobacillus_, _Streptococcus_, _Leuconostoc_ and _Pediococcus_. [Lemi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7042527) The review adds that natural fermentation can also allow food-poisoning organisms to grow, and recommends hygienic production, handling and storage. [Lemi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7042527)

## Tella: the household beer

Tella is the other half of the story. Reviews describe it as the most widely brewed and consumed alcoholic drink in Ethiopian households, made from barley malt (_bikil_), gesho and flours of millet, sorghum and teff, with roasted maize (_asharo_) setting its color. [Mulaw et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841419/) The process is long and staged. In the _Foods_ account, barley is soaked for about 24 hours to make malt, which is dried and ground and fermented with gesho for two days. A millet, sorghum or teff dough is baked into bread and added, and the mixture ferments in a sealed vessel for 5 to 7 days. Roasted maize flour is then added and fermented for 10 to 20 days, and finally water is added, the mixture rests five or six hours, and it is filtered. [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/)

Tella is lighter than tej: 3.98 to 6.48 percent by volume across localities in the _Foods_ review, 4 to 6 percent in the 2025 review, and 2 to 8 percent in a 2024 review in _Food Science & Nutrition_. [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/); [Mulaw et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841419/); [Alemu and Kuyu, Food Science & Nutrition 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077225/) It keeps only about 5 to 7 days at room temperature because household production is not aseptic. [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/) The 2024 review states that women predominantly prepare tella and that it is relatively rare for men to have the skill. [Alemu and Kuyu 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077225/) Methods vary by ethnic group and locality, and the drink is consumed in Amhara, Tigray, Oromia, Addis Ababa and the southern regions. [Mulaw et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841419/)

Neither drink should be confused with _areki_, a distilled spirit made from a fermented mash, which one study cited in the 2024 review measured at an average of about 37 percent alcohol across five collection sites. [Alemu and Kuyu 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077225/)

## Tej houses and social life

Tej is served in a _tejbet_, a small bar specializing in it, often in a flask-shaped pitcher called a _berele_. [Wikipedia: Tej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej); [Roads & Kingdoms](https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2016/everymans-drink-descended-ancient-royal-libations/) Roads & Kingdoms calls it "the drink of the everyman," especially at festivals, and describes honey wine as historically the drink of Ethiopian nobility. [Roads & Kingdoms](https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2016/everymans-drink-descended-ancient-royal-libations/) The _Foods_ review likewise says tej was once produced and consumed only for cultural festivities and royal families, and is now popular in rural, semi-urban and urban areas. [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/) Wikipedia notes that the claim of a purely aristocratic past is flagged as contradictory, so the history is best treated as uncertain. [Wikipedia: Tej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej) Wikipedia also lists weddings and the festivals of Enkutatash, Timkat, Fasika and Meskel as typical occasions. [Wikipedia: Tej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej) Fermented drinks are tied to marriage, naming and rain-making ceremonies, festivals, gatherings and burial rites in the 2020 microbiology review. [Lemi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7042527) Tej bars are also a traditional stage for minstrels; see [the azmari tradition](/en/music/azmari-tradition).

## Quality, safety and economics

The same reviews that celebrate these drinks are frank about their problems. Household preparation often gives inconsistent quality, fermented beverages carry a significant risk of toxin contamination, and pesticide residues on malting barley can pass into the finished drink; the authors of the 2024 review recommend HACCP principles. [Alemu and Kuyu 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077225/) Wikipedia says tej is linked to alcoholism because of its cultural prominence and easy home production, and that studies have raised concerns about alcohol and methanol levels. [Wikipedia: Tej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tej) Because the government does not control locally brewed drinks, it is hard to estimate how much alcohol is made and drunk; one review puts all Ethiopian traditionally fermented alcoholic beverages at about eight million hectoliters a year. [Lemi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7042527); [Fentie et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7761231/) The 2024 review notes that these beverages support rural livelihoods but have mostly not been commercialized, largely because of perceived low quality. [Alemu and Kuyu 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077225/)

## Closing

Tej and tella are largely made at home, by hand and by season. They depend on local ingredients and patient fermentation, and Ethiopian researchers are studying challenges from consistency to safety.

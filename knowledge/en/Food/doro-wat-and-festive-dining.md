# Doro Wat and Festive Dining: The Crown of Ethiopian Culinary Tradition

_Doro wat_ (ዶሮ ወጥ) is a spicy chicken stew, usually cooked with whole hard-boiled eggs and served on injera. It is often described as Ethiopia's national dish. Because it takes many hours to make, it is reserved for special occasions rather than everyday meals. [Wikipedia: Wat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat_%28food%29) Above all, it is the dish that ends the long Orthodox fasts. On the eve of Fasika (Easter), families slaughter a chicken at about 6 p.m. and cook doro wat through the evening, then eat it after a midnight church service that breaks a fast of nearly two months. [Saveur: This Epic Meat Feast Is How Ethiopians Celebrate Easter](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/) It is also the traditional dish of Genna (Christmas) and of Enkutatash, the Ethiopian New Year. [NPR: A 40-Day Vegan Fast, Then, At Last, A January Christmas Feast](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/373834051) [Wikipedia: Enkutatash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enkutatash)

## What Makes a Wat

_Wat_ (ወጥ) is the Amharic word for a stew. In Oromo it is _ittoo_ and in Tigrinya _tsebhi_. Wat can be made with chicken, beef, lamb or vegetables, and is seasoned with the chili blend _berbere_ and _niter kibbeh_, a spiced clarified butter. [Wikipedia: Wat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat_%28food%29) Its defining technique comes at the start. Chopped onions are cooked slowly in a dry pot, with no fat or oil, until much of their moisture is gone. Only then is fat added. The onions break down and thicken the stew. [Wikipedia: Wat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat_%28food%29)

Berbere is a blend of dried red chiles, garlic and onion with a dozen or so other spices, such as fenugreek and cardamom. It gives doro wat its deep red color and a layered heat that is tangy rather than scorching. [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/) The dish depends so heavily on its base that one Addis Ababa chef told Saveur: "Many people think of doro wat as a chicken dish, but it's really an onion one." [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/)

## How Doro Wat Is Made

Accounts of the method agree on its outline and differ on the details. That variety is itself a sign of a dish cooked by feel in each household. One Addis Ababa cook, preparing the Easter pot for Saveur, stirred 5 pounds (about 2.3 kilograms) of finely chopped red onions for 90 minutes before adding anything else. [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/) A cook in Nairobi told NPR that the nine pounds of onions she uses take four to five hours to become tender. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/373834051) A former London restaurateur interviewed by South Africa's Mail & Guardian used three kilograms of onions. She fried them for up to two hours, then added berbere, spiced butter and water little by little for at least another hour. [Mail & Guardian: What? You can't cook doro wot?](https://mg.co.za/article/2012-06-16-what-doro-wot) "A good cook just knows," she said, explaining why the recipe has no fixed quantities. [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/article/2012-06-16-what-doro-wot)

The chicken is skinned, washed repeatedly and soaked in water with lemon and salt. In the Mail & Guardian account it then simmers in the finished sauce for about an hour and a half. [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/article/2012-06-16-what-doro-wot) Peeled whole hard-boiled eggs go into the pot, where they turn a deep burgundy. [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/) Wikipedia, citing the sociologist Donald Levine, puts the whole preparation at roughly ten hours. [Wikipedia: Wat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat_%28food%29)

## Twelve Pieces, Twelve Eggs and Their Meanings

The cutting of the chicken carries symbolic weight, though households differ. In the Easter kitchen described by Saveur, the chicken was cut into "twelve precisely cut pieces", and a dozen eggs were added to represent the apostles. [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/) The Nairobi cook told NPR that Christmas doro wat calls for a rooster, not a hen, carved into exactly 12 pieces for the 12 disciples. Each wing is cut in two, and the neck and back complete the count. She also adds 12 eggs, which some say stand for eternity because an egg has no beginning or end. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/373834051) The Mail & Guardian's teacher, by contrast, worked from "seven good pieces of meat", two legs, breast, thighs and back, with the wings, neck and kidneys mixed into the sauce, and she used seven eggs. [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/article/2012-06-16-what-doro-wot)

Doro wat is also bound up with expectations about gender. The Mail & Guardian piece, written by an Ethiopian woman, opens with her godmother's warning: "You will never find a husband if you cannot cook doro wot!" The writer reports hearing the same idea from a taxi driver and a flatmate. In the household she visited, only men who had kept the fast were allowed to slaughter the Easter chicken. [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/article/2012-06-16-what-doro-wot) These are reported customs and sayings, not universal rules, and they vary by family and region.

## The Post-Fast Feast

Doro wat's place at the table makes sense only against the fasting calendar. The Ethiopian Orthodox Church keeps a 55-day Lenten fast, during which meat, milk, butter and eggs are avoided. The fast is broken with a joyful meal after the midnight Easter Mass, at about 3 a.m. [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church: Worship](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/ethiopian/worship.html) Saveur's account counts 56 days of Lent and "some 200 days" of fasting in the year. The church's own description says there are about 250 fast days, of which an ordinary believer keeps about 180. [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/) [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church: Worship](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/ethiopian/worship.html) Before Christmas, the Fast of the Prophets lasts 43 days. NPR's Nairobi report describes it as a 40-day vegan fast. [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gena) [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/373834051)

In the family Saveur followed, the doro wat was only tasted when the fast broke at 3 a.m. It was kept as the centerpiece of Easter lunch, alongside lamb slaughtered at dawn, beef _tibs_, raw beef, a goat stew, and _tej_ (honey wine) for guests. [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/) Wikipedia's Fasika entry describes the same rhythm: a chicken slaughtered for the night meal and a sheep in the morning. [Wikipedia: Fasika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasika) The meal ended, as festive meals usually do, with a three-round coffee ceremony. [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/meat-feast-ethiopian-easter/)

## Doro Wat Beyond the Orthodox Table

Doro wat is not only a Christian dish. Among the Beta Israel (Ethiopian Jews), a version called _sanbat wat_ ("Sabbath wat") is a traditional Shabbat dish. To avoid mixing meat and dairy, it is cooked with vegetable oil or an oil-based form of spiced butter instead of niter kibbeh. [Wikipedia: Wat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat_%28food%29) Rising costs are a modern pressure on the dish. In 2012 the Mail & Guardian's cook reported paying 200 birr for an Easter chicken, almost ten times the price of seven years earlier. [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/article/2012-06-16-what-doro-wot)

Doro wat is always eaten on injera, the fermented teff flatbread described in [[injera-and-teff]]. For the fasts and feasts that set its calendar, see [[ethiopian-religious-festivals]].

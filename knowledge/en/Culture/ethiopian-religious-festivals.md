# The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and Its Major Festivals

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church (የኢትዮጵያ ኦርቶዶክስ ተዋሕዶ ቤተ ክርስቲያን) is one of the oldest churches in the world. Christianity became the state religion of the Kingdom of Aksum around 330, under King Ezana. [CNEWA: The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church](https://cnewa.org/eastern-christian-churches/oriental-orthodox-churches/the-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo-church/) Its yearly festivals, above all Timkat (Epiphany), Meskel, Genna (Christmas) and Fasika (Easter), are acts of worship and also great gatherings of family, neighbors and nation. Two of them are on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity: Meskel, inscribed in 2013, and Timkat, inscribed in 2019. [UNESCO: Meskel](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/commemoration-feast-of-the-finding-of-the-true-holy-cross-of-christ-00858) [UNESCO: Ethiopian epiphany](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ethiopian-epiphany-01491) The church is the largest of Ethiopia's religious communities, but not the only one, and this article closes with the country's other major religious calendars.

## Origins: From the Kingdom of Aksum

According to church tradition, Christianity was brought to Aksum by Frumentius, a Roman citizen from Tyre who was shipwrecked on the Red Sea coast. He won the confidence of the Aksumite court and converted the king's son, Ezana. Frumentius was later ordained a bishop by Athanasius of Alexandria and returned to Ethiopia; Ethiopians remember him as Abba Salama. [CNEWA](https://cnewa.org/eastern-christian-churches/oriental-orthodox-churches/the-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo-church/) Around 480, the "Nine Saints", monks who by tradition came from Rome, Constantinople and Syria, arrived and translated the Bible and other religious works into Ge'ez. [CNEWA](https://cnewa.org/eastern-christian-churches/oriental-orthodox-churches/the-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo-church/)

_Tewahedo_ is a Ge'ez word meaning "being made one" or "unified". It expresses the church's teaching that Christ's humanity and divinity are united in a single nature. [CNEWA](https://cnewa.org/eastern-christian-churches/oriental-orthodox-churches/the-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo-church/) For centuries the church's head was appointed from the Coptic Church in Egypt. In 1959 the Coptic Patriarchate confirmed Abuna Basilios as the first Patriarch of the Ethiopian church, establishing its autonomy. Ge'ez remains the historic language of the liturgy, though Amharic translations are increasingly used in parishes. [CNEWA](https://cnewa.org/eastern-christian-churches/oriental-orthodox-churches/the-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo-church/)

## Fasting and the Calendar

The church year moves through a rhythm of fasts and feasts. The church's own description counts about 250 fast days a year, of which an ordinary believer may keep about 180. During fasts, believers abstain from meat, milk, butter and eggs, and no food is taken before noon. [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church: Worship](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/ethiopian/worship.html) The church lists seven official fasting periods:

- every Wednesday and Friday, except in the 50 days after Easter
- the 55-day Lenten fast
- the three-day Fast of Nineveh
- the vigils before Christmas and Epiphany
- the Fast of the Apostles, which lasts 14 to 44 days depending on the date of Easter
- the 43-day Fast of the Prophets before Christmas
- the 15-day Fast of the Assumption in August

The Fasts of the Apostles and of the Prophets are compulsory only for clergy, though many lay people keep them. Children under seven, pregnant women, the seriously ill and travelers are exempt. [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church: Worship](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/ethiopian/worship.html)

The feasts follow Ethiopia's own calendar. It has twelve months of 30 days each and a thirteenth month, Pagume, of five days, or six in a leap year. It runs seven to eight years behind the Gregorian calendar because it calculates the date of Jesus' birth differently. [timeanddate.com: The Ethiopian Calendar](https://www.timeanddate.com/calendar/ethiopia-calendar.html) The new year, Enkutatash, falls on Meskerem 1, which is September 11, or September 12 in the year before a Gregorian leap year. [Wikipedia: Enkutatash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enkutatash)

## Timkat

Timkat (ጥምቀት) commemorates the baptism of Jesus by John the Baptist in the River Jordan. It falls on Tir 11, usually January 19, or January 20 in a leap year. [Wikipedia: Timkat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timkat) Every Ethiopian church keeps a _tabot_, a replica of the Ark of the Covenant, and without it no service can be held. When a tabot leaves the church in procession, as it does at Epiphany, it is covered with cloth and people bow as it passes. [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church: Worship](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/ethiopian/worship.html)

On the eve, called Ketera (January 18), each parish escorts its tabot, carried by a priest, to a pool, river or artificial reservoir known as the Timkete-Bahir. The name Ketera refers to the damming of water so that it can be blessed. Crowds spend the night in prayer, hymns and the Eucharistic liturgy. [UNESCO: Ethiopian epiphany](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ethiopian-epiphany-01491) The next morning, after rituals that begin before sunrise, blessed water is sprinkled on the congregation, and some participants step into the water to renew their baptismal vows. [Wikipedia: Timkat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timkat) Hundreds of thousands take part. Around 10 a.m. each tabot begins its procession back to its church, accompanied by traditional and religious songs. [UNESCO: Ethiopian epiphany](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ethiopian-epiphany-01491)

## Meskel and the Demera Bonfire

Meskel (መስቀል, "cross") commemorates the finding of the True Cross by Empress Helena in the 4th century. The feast falls on Meskerem 17, which is September 27, or September 28 in leap years. [Wikipedia: Meskel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskel) In the church's account, Helena could not find the place where the Cross was buried until an old man advised her to pile wood, add incense, burn it and dig where the smoke drifted. [Mahibere Kidusan: The Holy Cross and its Celebration](https://eotcmk.org/e/the-holy-cross-and-its-celebration/)

That story is re-enacted with the _Demera_, a bonfire built on the eve of the feast, Meskerem 16 (September 26). People bring torches of twigs called _chibo_ and wood to Meskel Square in Addis Ababa. Priests pray in front of the pyre while a procession circles it, singing that Meskerem has dawned and the flowers have bloomed. In Addis Ababa and some other places the Demera is lit on the evening of the 16th; elsewhere it is lit on the morning of the 17th. Afterward, people mark crosses on their foreheads with its ashes. [Mahibere Kidusan](https://eotcmk.org/e/the-holy-cross-and-its-celebration/)

UNESCO describes the Demera in Meskel Square as a conical pyre decorated with green grass and Abyssinian daisies, which symbolize the new year. Colorfully dressed priests chant and dance in front of it before the Patriarch lights it, watched by hundreds of thousands of people. [UNESCO: Meskel](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/commemoration-feast-of-the-finding-of-the-true-holy-cross-of-christ-00858) The feast is celebrated across Ethiopia regardless of age, gender, language or ethnicity. Personal quarrels are expected to be settled beforehand, and migrant workers return home for it, bringing money and news from the cities to rural areas. [UNESCO: Meskel](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/commemoration-feast-of-the-finding-of-the-true-holy-cross-of-christ-00858)

## Genna and Fasika

Genna (ገና, Christmas) falls on Tahsas 29, January 7 in the Gregorian calendar. Protestant and Catholic Ethiopians celebrate it as well. Orthodox believers prepare with the 43-day Fast of the Prophets, and on Christmas Eve they attend an overnight service, which Wikipedia describes as running from about 6 p.m. to 3 a.m. [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gena) Many wear the white cotton _netela_ shawl, and the feast draws pilgrims to the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela. Christmas Day is also associated with _gena_, a hockey-like game played with curved sticks and a wooden ball. [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gena)

Fasika (ፋሲካ, Easter), also called Tinsae ("resurrection"), is the most important feast of the church year. It follows the Eastern method of calculating Easter, so it often falls after Western Easter. [Wikipedia: Fasika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasika) Easter Mass is celebrated at 1 a.m., and the 55-day Lenten fast is broken with a joyful meal after the midnight Mass, at about 3 a.m. [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church: Worship](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/ethiopian/worship.html) The 50 days that follow are free of fasting and prostrations. [Wikipedia: Fasika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasika)

## Ethiopia's Other Religious Calendars

Orthodox feasts are the most visible, but Ethiopia is religiously diverse. According to the 2007 census, the most recent available, about 44% of Ethiopians follow the Orthodox Tewahedo Church, 34% are Sunni Muslims and 19% belong to evangelical and Pentecostal churches. [US State Department: 2023 Report on International Religious Freedom, Ethiopia](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2111862.html) Muslim festivals are national occasions too. On Eid al-Fitr in March 2025, thousands of worshippers gathered at Addis Ababa Stadium for prayers, and large celebrations were held in Harar, Jinka, Arba Minch and Hosanna. [ENA: Nationwide celebrations mark 1446th Eid al-Fitr](https://www.ena.et/web/eng/w/eng_6284507)

Feast days are also when families cook their richest dishes and gather around the coffee pot. For the ceremony that closes almost every festive meal, see [[ethiopian-coffee-culture]]; for the Easter and Christmas centerpiece, see [[doro-wat-and-festive-dining]].

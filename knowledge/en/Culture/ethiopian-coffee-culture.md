# Ethiopian Coffee Culture and the Coffee Ceremony

For Ethiopians, coffee (_buna_, ቡና) is a daily ritual that expresses hospitality, neighborliness and family bonds. Wild Arabica coffee (_Coffea arabica_) grows in the mountain forests of south-western Ethiopia, and UNESCO describes the Kafa Zone as the centre of origin and genetic diversity of the species. [UNESCO MAB: Kafa Biosphere Reserve](https://www.unesco.org/en/mab/kafa) At home, the coffee ceremony is usually led by a woman and runs through three rounds of brewing from the same grounds; the whole process takes about an hour, and it is a time for news, gossip, planning and reconciliation. [The Guardian: 'It's rude not to offer three cups'](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) Coffee is also the country's largest export, earning a record of more than USD 2.65 billion in the 2024/25 fiscal year. [ENA: Record-breaking coffee export earnings](https://www.ena.et/web/eng/w/eng_7781557)

## Coffee's Birthplace and Its Legends

The forests of Kafa, in south-western Ethiopia, are considered the birthplace of wild Arabica. UNESCO designated them a biosphere reserve in 2010, covering about 760,000 hectares, and reports close to 5,000 wild coffee varieties there. [UNESCO MAB: Kafa Biosphere Reserve](https://www.unesco.org/en/mab/kafa) The World Bank describes the reserve as a seed bank of wild Arabica, holding thousands of genetic variants that breeders have used to find resistance to coffee berry disease. [World Bank: Why biodiversity matters to your daily cup of coffee](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2020/06/04/why-biodiversity-matters-to-your-daily-cup-of-coffee)

The best-known story of coffee's discovery concerns a goatherd named Kaldi, who noticed his goats becoming unusually lively after eating the red berries. Britannica presents this as "one of many legends" about coffee's discovery, not as documented history. [Britannica: History of coffee](https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-coffee) In Kafa today, local tellers add that priests then chewed the beans to stay awake through long prayers, and they point out that the Arabic word for coffee, _qahwa_, sounds like "Kafa". The Guardian, reporting from the region, calls the story "probably apocryphal". [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) The firmer history begins across the Red Sea. Coffee drinking became popular in Yemen in the 15th century, where the Shadhiliyya Sufi order adopted it. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee)

## The Coffee Ceremony, Step by Step

The ceremony is usually performed by the woman of the household and is considered an honor. Loose grass, often with small yellow flowers, is spread on the floor where it takes place. [Wikipedia: Coffee ceremony of Ethiopia and Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_ceremony_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea) The green beans are roasted over coals in a flat pan, and the smoking pan is carried round so that guests can waft the aroma toward themselves. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee) The roasted beans are ground by hand in a wooden mortar, the _mukecha_, and brewed in a _jebena_ (ጀበና), a clay pot with a round base, a long neck and a spout. [Wikipedia: Coffee ceremony of Ethiopia and Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_ceremony_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea)

The coffee is poured from a height into small handleless cups. The oldest and most respected guests are served first. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee) The host pours in one unbroken stream from about one foot above a tray of cups until each is full. [Wikipedia: Coffee ceremony of Ethiopia and Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_ceremony_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea) Sugar is the usual addition. In rural areas where sugar is scarce, a pinch of salt may be used instead, and some people dip a sprig of _tenadam_ (rue) into the cup. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee)

Customs vary by region. In Kafa, many people drink their coffee unsweetened or with salt. On special occasions, butter infused with basil, oregano and cardamom is stirred in. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) Snacks such as popcorn, peanuts, _himbasha_ bread or roasted grain (_kolo_) are served alongside. [Wikipedia: Coffee ceremony of Ethiopia and Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_ceremony_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea)

## Abol, Tona, Baraka

The grounds are brewed three times, and each round has its own name. In Amharic the rounds are _abol_ (አቦል), _tona_ (ቶና) and _baraka_ (በረካ). In Tigrinya they are _awel_, _kale'i_ and _baraka_. [Wikipedia: Coffee ceremony of Ethiopia and Eritrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_ceremony_of_Ethiopia_and_Eritrea) UNESCO notes that these words come from Arabic and mean "first", "second" and "blessing", a sign of the ceremony's religious roots. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee) The leader of the ceremony speaks blessings as the coffee is served. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee)

It is generally considered rude not to offer all three cups. Incense is burned while the coffee brews; its smell is thought to improve the taste of the coffee, and some believe it keeps bad spirits away. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together)

## A Pillar of Social Life

Because the ceremony takes so long, neighbors often take turns hosting. In one Kafa village described by the Guardian, one household makes the morning round, another the lunchtime round, and in the evening everyone gathers at a third home. The Guardian notes that systems like this are repeated in various forms in millions of homes across the country. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together) The same report describes coffee ceremonies as a central part of weddings, funerals and religious celebrations. It also notes that women brewing coffee on charcoal stoves can be found on almost every street corner in Ethiopia's cities. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together)

Coffee was not always shared across religious lines. According to UNESCO, coffee was historically drunk mainly by Muslims in Ethiopia, and Christians were forbidden to drink it until the end of the 19th century. Emperor Menelik II, who reigned from 1889 to 1913, drank coffee openly and encouraged his Christian subjects to do the same. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee) The ceremony itself has roots in Sufi mysticism. Its utensils are seen as carrying spiritual power, which helps explain why its last cup is called "blessing". [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee)

## From Culture to Economy

Ethiopia is Africa's biggest coffee producer and the world's fifth-largest exporter, according to the World Bank. [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2020/06/04/why-biodiversity-matters-to-your-daily-cup-of-coffee) Ethiopians themselves are major consumers: UNESCO puts domestic consumption at about 40% of national production. [UNESCO: Ethiopia, the home of coffee](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethiopia-home-coffee)

Estimates of how many people depend on coffee vary. The World Bank says an estimated 15 million Ethiopians rely on it for their livelihoods. [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2020/06/04/why-biodiversity-matters-to-your-daily-cup-of-coffee) The Guardian counts about 5 million coffee-growing farmers and says coffee employs about 25% of the population directly or indirectly. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2024/apr/23/its-rude-not-to-offer-three-cups-the-lengthy-beloved-coffee-rituals-binding-ethiopians-together)

Export earnings have risen sharply. In the 2024/25 Ethiopian fiscal year, which ended in July 2025 (2017 E.C.), the government reported exports of about 470,000 tonnes worth more than USD 2.65 billion, a record. [ENA](https://www.ena.et/web/eng/w/eng_7781557)

The wild genetic base behind this trade is at risk. A 2019 study led by scientists at the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew projected that, under climate change, wild Arabica populations could fall by 50% or more by 2088. The study assessed wild Arabica as Endangered under IUCN Red List criteria, whereas without climate change it would count as Least Concern. [Moat, Gole & Davis, Global Change Biology (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.14341) Only about 4% of the potential forest area for wild Arabica in Ethiopia and South Sudan lies inside protected areas. [Mongabay: 60% of wild coffee species threatened with extinction](https://news.mongabay.com/2019/01/coffee-in-trouble-60-of-wild-coffee-species-threatened-with-extinction/) After Kafa was designated a biosphere reserve, the World Bank reports, deforestation dropped, particularly in the protected core zone. [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2020/06/04/why-biodiversity-matters-to-your-daily-cup-of-coffee)

The coffee ceremony runs through Ethiopia's religious calendar too. For the feasts at which families gather over coffee, see [[ethiopian-religious-festivals]].

# Harar Jugol: The Walled City and Its Harari Heritage

## 30-second overview

Harar, in eastern Ethiopia, is a walled town of crowded alleys, mosques, shrines and tall-roomed courtyard houses. Its old core, called Jugol (ጀጎል), was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2006 (1998 in the Ethiopian calendar) as "Harar Jugol, the Fortified Historic Town." [UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Harar Jugol](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189) It is the historic home of the Harari people and a long-standing center of Islamic learning in eastern Ethiopia. The city is also known for something modern visitors often come to see first, its night-time hyena feeders, a custom whose age is disputed.

## Where it is and who lives there

Harar is the capital of the Harari Region. Wikipedia describes the Harari as the indigenous people of the city, which is also called Harar-Gey in their language. [Wikipedia: Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar) The Wikipedia article gives a census count of 99,368 residents in 2007 and an estimate of 153,000 in 2021; those figures are for the whole city, not only the walled town. [Wikipedia: Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar) The sources do not agree on when the city was founded. Wikipedia notes that some accounts credit Qurayshi settlers in the late 7th century, others a local creation in the 10th century, and a semi-legendary text credits the saint Abadir Umar ar-Rida and his followers with founding it around 1216. History Hit also dates Abadir's arrival to about 1216. [Wikipedia: Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar); [History Hit: Harar Jugol](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/harar-jugol/) What can be said safely is that Harar was a Muslim town and a center of Islamic learning for centuries before it became part of the Ethiopian state.

## From emirate to Ethiopia

According to History Hit, Harar was the capital of the Harari Kingdom from 1520 to 1568, which it calls the town's "Golden Age," and from the late 16th to the 19th century it served as a trade hub between the coast and the highlands. [History Hit: Harar Jugol](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/harar-jugol/) Wikipedia dates the independent Emirate of Harar to the 17th century under Ali ibn Da'ud. Egypt occupied the city in the late 19th century (History Hit gives 1875), and in 1887 Menelik II defeated Emir Abdullahi at the Battle of Chelenqo and took Harar. Ras Makonnen then governed the region. [Wikipedia: Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar) The French-built railway from Addis Ababa to Djibouti passed Harar by, which History Hit says hurt its commerce. [History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/harar-jugol/) That claim comes from a single, popular source and is worth treating with care.

One foreign resident stands out in local memory. The French poet Arthur Rimbaud lived in Harar in the 1880s as a trading agent. [Wikipedia: Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar) Sources describe the house associated with him differently: Wikipedia calls it a museum, while the Islamic Architectural Heritage site says it holds a municipal library and cultural center. [Islamic Architectural Heritage: Jugol (Harar)](https://islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/?p=1233)

## The walls and the five gates

The wall that encloses the old town is the feature that gives Jugol its name. Most sources date it to the 16th century, and Wikipedia says it was largely built then under Nur ibn Mujahid. [Wikipedia: Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar); [Islamic Architectural Heritage](https://islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/?p=1233) History Hit states more loosely that the walls were built "in the 13th and 16th centuries." [History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/harar-jugol/) The size of the walled area is also reported differently, at about 60 hectares by Wikipedia and 48 hectares in a summary of UNESCO's inscription data. The old city has five ancient gates: Assum Bari, Argobba Bari, Suqutat Bari, Badro Bari and Asmadin Bari. [Wikipedia: Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar) Discover Wildlife notes that the five human gates closed at sunset, while the walls also include small openings for another purpose, described under the hyenas below. [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/mammals/ethiopian-city-hyenas-feeding)

## Mosques, shrines and the Muslim town

Counts of religious buildings vary by source and date. A summary of UNESCO's listing gives 82 mosques and 102 shrines, with three of the mosques dating from the 10th century; the Islamic Architectural Heritage site gives 82 mosques and "more than 90" shrines, while History Hit lists both 82 and, elsewhere on the same page, 110 mosques. [UNESCO World Heritage Centre](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189); [Islamic Architectural Heritage](https://islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/?p=1233); [History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/harar-jugol/) This article therefore says "about 82 mosques" and treats the shrine total as between 90 and 102. The UNESCO figures are quoted as summarized in secondary listings, so check the official inscription documents before relying on them.

Harar is regarded as one of the holy cities of Islam, partly because, according to tradition, Ethiopia's kingdom welcomed the Prophet's followers when they fled Arabia. [Islamic Architectural Heritage](https://islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/?p=1233) Popular sources often call it Islam's "fourth holiest city"; that ranking is informal, and no formal list is cited here. The same source notes that after the 1887 conquest the town's main central mosque was replaced by a circular Christian church, and History Hit names the Medhane Alem Cathedral among the notable buildings. [Islamic Architectural Heritage](https://islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/?p=1233); [History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/harar-jugol/)

## Houses, markets and crafts

The traditional Harari house is, in the words of the Islamic Architectural Heritage page, "the most spectacular part of the heritage." Its form is distinctive and unlike typical Muslim domestic layouts, though "reminiscent of the coastal Arab architecture." Indian merchants added houses in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with wooden verandas, which also form part of the heritage. [Islamic Architectural Heritage](https://islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/?p=1233) History Hit describes the town's historic townhouses as a blend of African and Islamic traditions. [History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/harar-jugol/)

Harar also gave Ethiopia one of its best-known household objects. The _mesob_, a woven basket used to store injera, is described as a Harari craft, made of locally grown grass and palm leaves and partly dyed. Wikipedia reports that an early form was noted in the 16th century by the Portuguese traveler Francisco Álvares, that the most prominent mesob is of Harar origin, and that in the mid-1970s Amhara patrons recruited Harari artisans to make a version blending Abyssinian and Harari designs. [Wikipedia: Mesob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesob) For the mesob at the table, see [Gursha, the mesob and Ethiopian hospitality](/en/lifestyle/gursha-mesob-and-hospitality).

## Heritage status

UNESCO inscribed the site under criteria ii, iii, iv and v, and the Islamic Architectural Heritage page records that Harar has been an Ethiopian national heritage site since 1974, with an inventory and conservation policy handled by the national authority (ARCCH, 1976) and a Jugol Heritage Conservation Office set up in 2003 to liaise with local authorities. [Islamic Architectural Heritage](https://islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/?p=1233) The page also says that Harar "preserved almost completely its authenticity and integrity," a judgment from the inscription documents rather than an independent survey.

## The hyena feeders

Harar's best-known modern ritual is the feeding of wild spotted hyenas outside the walls. Wikipedia says written records show hyenas have lived in Harar for at least 500 years, where they clean the city by eating organic waste. [Wikipedia: Spotted hyenas in Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_hyenas_in_Harar)

The age of the feeding custom is genuinely contested. Wikipedia says regular feeding did not start until the 1960s, when a farmer began it to keep hyenas from his livestock and his descendants continued. [Wikipedia: Spotted hyenas in Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_hyenas_in_Harar) Discover Wildlife quotes a local expert, Hailu Gashew, who dates feeding to about 300 years ago, tied to the Ashura ceremony and shrouded in legend. [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/mammals/ethiopian-city-hyenas-feeding) Folklore recorded by Wikipedia says porridge made with pure butter is left for the hyenas on Ashura, and how much they eat is read as an omen: more than half predicts a prosperous year, while refusing it or eating all of it sends people to pray at shrines. [Wikipedia: Spotted hyenas in Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_hyenas_in_Harar) Discover Wildlife adds that a king ordered small gates cut into the walls so hyenas could enter after dark. [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/mammals/ethiopian-city-hyenas-feeding)

Today's feeders are known as hyena men. National Geographic profiles Abbas Yusuf, who learned the practice from his father, Yusuf Mume Salleh, and who names the hyenas and calls them with a special cry at dusk, partly to entertain tourists. [National Geographic: This man lives with hyenas](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/photography/proof/2017/08/this-man-lives-with-hyenas/) Discover Wildlife says the practice grew after wide media attention, including the BBC's _Planet Earth II_, and warns that regional development is shrinking the animals' range. [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/mammals/ethiopian-city-hyenas-feeding) Because most accounts come from tourism journalism, they should be read as a picture of a visitor attraction as well as a local custom.

## Closing

Harar Jugol is a place where the layers are visible: a medieval Muslim town, a nineteenth-century frontier, a modern regional capital, and a heritage site with its own conservation office. It is best read through its walls, doors and daily life, and not only through its hyenas.

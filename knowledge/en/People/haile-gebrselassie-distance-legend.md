# Haile Gebrselassie: Two Olympic Golds and the First Sub-2:04 Marathon

## 30-second overview

Haile Gebrselassie (ኃይሌ ገብረ ሥላሴ) was born on 18 April 1973 in Asella, in what is now the Oromia Region ([Olympedia: Haile Gebrselassie](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67405)). Over a competitive career that ended in 2015 he won two Olympic 10,000 m titles, took a string of world championship gold medals, and set dozens of world records or world bests. In 2008 he became the first person to run a marathon in under two hours and four minutes. This article is limited to documented sporting facts and to his own public statements about his retirement. Where sources count his records differently, we give the range.

## Early Success

World Athletics records that he won the 5,000 m and 10,000 m double at the 1992 World Junior Championships ([World Athletics: Haile Gebrselassie retires](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/haile-gebrselassie-retires)). Olympedia, the Olympic results database, says he won the 10,000 m world title four times in a row, in 1993, 1995, 1997 and 1999, and the half-marathon title in 2001, and added four world indoor titles: the 3,000 m in 1997, 1999 and 2003 and the 1,500 m in 1999 ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67405)). World Athletics counts these as nine world titles in total ([World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/haile-gebrselassie-retires)).

## The Olympics

His 10,000 m gold at the 1996 Atlanta Games came in an Olympic record of 27:07.34, and he repeated as champion in Sydney in 2000. In Athens in 2004 he finished fifth in the same event, and in Beijing in 2008 sixth. Olympedia adds that he competed at four Olympics and carried Ethiopia's flag at the 2012 Opening Ceremony ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67405)). He did not win a medal after 2000, which is a reminder that his dominance on the track was concentrated in the 1990s.

## Records

Counts of his world records vary because sources distinguish official records from "world bests," and indoor from outdoor marks.

- On the day of his 2008 Berlin run, World Athletics said he had set "27 World records or World bests" ([World Athletics: Gebrselassie again](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/flash-gebrselassie-again-20359-world-record)).
- An opinion piece in the AIMS magazine _Distance Running_ by David Martin counted 18 IAAF-approved world records between 1994 and 2007 ([Martin: A class of his own](https://www.aims-worldrunning.org/articles/1392-a-class-of-his-own.html)).
- At his 2015 retirement, World Athletics credited him with 15 official outdoor world records and five world indoor records, and said he still held the world records for 20,000 m and the one-hour run ([World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/haile-gebrselassie-retires)). That statement was accurate as of May 2015; we have not checked whether those marks have since been broken.

Two of his best-known track performances came within two weeks in 1998. At Hengelo he ran the 10,000 m in 26:22.75, and in Helsinki he ran 12:39.36 for 5,000 m ([Martin](https://www.aims-worldrunning.org/articles/1392-a-class-of-his-own.html)); Olympedia lists the same two personal bests and dates them to 1998 ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67405)).

## The Marathon

On 30 September 2007 he won the Berlin Marathon in 2:04:26, 29 seconds faster than Paul Tergat's 2:04:55 ([Martin](https://www.aims-worldrunning.org/articles/1392-a-class-of-his-own.html)). A year later, on 28 September 2008, he won again in 2:03:59, taking 27 seconds off his own mark; World Athletics called the performance pending ratification at the time and described him as the first man under 2:04 ([World Athletics: Gebrselassie again](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/flash-gebrselassie-again-20359-world-record)). The World Marathon Majors report says he went through halfway in 62:04, beat Kenya's James Kwambai, who ran 2:05:36, by about 1 minute 37 seconds, and was helped by four Kenyan pacemakers. Gebrselassie called Berlin "my lucky city" and said "everything was perfect, the weather, the pacemakers" ([World Marathon Majors: Haile shatters own world record in Berlin](https://www.worldmarathonmajors.com/content-hub/haile-shatters-own-world-record-in-berlin)). Olympedia notes he won Berlin four times, from 2006 to 2009 ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67405)).

His marathon mark was later bettered by other runners, and we have not cited a source for the current record, so this article says nothing about who holds it today.

## Retirement and Business

He ended his competitive career on 10 May 2015 after finishing 16th at the Great Manchester Run. World Athletics quotes him: "I'm retiring from competitive running, not from running," and "You cannot stop running, this is my life" ([World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/haile-gebrselassie-retires)). The National reported that he planned to remain a "running ambassador," to make one more appearance at the Great Scottish Run that October, and to focus on business interests in Ethiopia including real estate, hotels, a coffee plantation and a car distribution company. It added that he had previously expressed interest in entering politics, and that he described his "next big dream" as promoting development in his home country ([The National: Haile Gebrselassie retiring, not from running but from competing](https://thenationalnews.com/sport/haile-gebrselassie-retiring-not-from-running-but-from-competing-1.113244)). The business details come from his own stated plans in 2015, not from audited accounts, and we have not verified the scale of those ventures.

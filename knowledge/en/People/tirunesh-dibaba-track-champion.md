# Tirunesh Dibaba: Three Olympic Golds and the 5,000-10,000 m Double

## 30-second overview

Tirunesh Dibaba Keneni (ጥሩነሽ ዲባባ) was born in Bekoji, in the Oromia Region, and is one of the most decorated women in distance running ([Olympedia: Tirunesh Dibaba](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/104973)). She won three Olympic golds, in the 5,000 m and 10,000 m at Beijing in 2008 and in the 10,000 m again at London in 2012, and she is the only woman to have won the 10,000 m at two consecutive Olympics ([World Athletics: Dibaba is back writing Olympic history again](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/dibaba-is-back-writing-olympic-history-again-1)). This article uses race reports and the Olympic results database, and it gives ranges where sources disagree.

## Origins

Olympedia gives her birth date as 1 October 1985 in Bekoji, but notes that Olympic entry lists for 2008, 2012 and 2016 show 1 June; it explains that Ethiopia had no formal birth registration, so the date is hard to verify. It records her family ties to the sport: her sisters Ejegayehu Dibaba, who won 10,000 m silver at the 2004 Olympics, and Genzebe Dibaba; her husband Sileshi Sihine, a 10,000 m silver medalist in 2004 and 2008; and her cousin Derartu Tulu ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/104973)). World Athletics describes Tulu as her cousin and mentor ([World Athletics: London 2012 event report](https://worldathletics.org/news/report/london-2012-event-report-womens-10000m-fi)). The uncertainty about her birth date also affects how her age records are described: World Athletics calls her 17 when she won the 2003 World Championships 5,000 m, the youngest individual gold medalist in that event, while Olympedia gives her age as 18 years and 90 days ([World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/dibaba-is-back-writing-olympic-history-again-1); [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/104973)).

## World Championships

Olympedia counts nine world titles: 5,000 m in 2003 and 2005; 10,000 m in 2005, 2007 and 2013; and cross-country titles in the short course in 2005 and the long course in 2005, 2006 and 2008. In 2005 she became the first woman to win both the 5,000 m and 10,000 m at the same World Championships, and she is one of two women, with Sonia O'Sullivan, to win both short and long course cross-country titles at the same championships. She also took 10,000 m silver in 2017 ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/104973)).

## Beijing 2008

On 15 August 2008 she won the Olympic 10,000 m in 29:54.66, an Olympic and African record and then the second-fastest time in history. Turkey's Elvan Abeylegesse took silver in 29:56.34, and the two became the second and third women ever under 30 minutes. Shalane Flanagan of the United States took bronze with a North American record of 30:22.22. Dibaba said she hoped to break the 15-year-old world record of 29:31.86 held by Wang Junxia of China and added, "Next year I'm sure I'll do it" ([World Athletics: Dibaba smashes Olympic 10,000m record](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/dibaba-smashes-olympic-10000m-record-as-two-d)). Olympedia lists her 10,000 m personal best as that 2008 run and her 5,000 m best as 14:11.15, also from 2008 ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/104973)); the world record she spoke of was not hers in that race. World Athletics says she became the first woman to win both long-distance titles at one Games ([World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/dibaba-is-back-writing-olympic-history-again-1)).

## Injury and London 2012

After Beijing she was out of competition for more than a year with a stress fracture in her shin, and the Laureus World Sports Awards nominated her for Comeback of the Year in 2013, describing a 16-month absence ([Laureus: Tirunesh Dibaba](https://www.laureus.com/world-sports-awards/2013/comeback-of-the-year/tirunesh-dibaba)). Her return included a win at Madrid on New Year's Eve 2011 and a 10,000 m run of 30:24.39 in Eugene in June 2012 ([World Athletics: London 2012 event report](https://worldathletics.org/news/report/london-2012-event-report-womens-10000m-fi)); Laureus gives the Eugene race as the Prefontaine Classic.

On 3 August 2012 in London she won the 10,000 m in 30:20.75, ahead of Kenya's Sally Kipyego (30:26.37) and Vivian Cheruiyot (30:30.44). She became the first woman to win back-to-back Olympic 10,000 m titles, and Tulu, who won in 1992 and 2000, remains the only other woman with two titles in the event. She came to London as a reserve for the 5,000 m after the injury; she said afterward, "I have never been happier than today - this is even better than in Beijing." She later took bronze in the 5,000 m in London ([World Athletics: Dibaba is back writing Olympic history again](https://worldathletics.org/news/news/dibaba-is-back-writing-olympic-history-again-1)). Olympedia lists the 5,000 m bronze ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/104973)).

## Medal Totals and Later Career

Olympedia counts six Olympic medals: three golds (Beijing 5,000 m and 10,000 m; London 10,000 m) and three bronzes (Athens 2004 5,000 m; London 2012 5,000 m; Rio 2016 10,000 m). It adds that she carried the Ethiopian flag at the 2012 closing ceremony ([Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/104973)). Laureus says the London title made her one of seven Olympians with three individual track golds ([Laureus](https://www.laureus.com/world-sports-awards/2013/comeback-of-the-year/tirunesh-dibaba)).

## Marathon Years

Dibaba moved to the marathon after her track career. Marathon Handbook's athlete profile lists her results: third in London on 13 April 2014 in 2:20:35, second in London on 23 April 2017 in 2:17:56, which it gives as her personal best, and first in Chicago on 8 October 2017 in 2:18:31, her one major-marathon win on its list. It then shows a did-not-finish in London in April 2018, third in Berlin on 16 September 2018 in 2:18:55, and a did-not-finish in New York on 3 November 2024. [Marathon Handbook: Tirunesh Dibaba](https://marathonhandbook.com/athletes/tirunesh-dibaba/) The same profile lists no marathon in 2025 or 2026 and makes no statement about retirement, so her current status is not established here. This is a single results page with no byline or date, and it gives her birth date as 1 June 1985 where Olympedia gives 1 October 1985, so the figures should be checked against official race results before they are quoted elsewhere.

## Why She Matters

Dibaba belongs to a line of Ethiopian women, including Tulu and her own sisters, who made women's distance running a source of national pride. The reports used here show someone who dominated at 17 and returned after a long injury to win again, but they are race reports rather than analytical studies, so they say little about the training system behind her.

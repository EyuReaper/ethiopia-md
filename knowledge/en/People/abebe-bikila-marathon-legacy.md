# Abebe Bikila and the Marathon Legacy: The Barefoot Pioneer of African Athletics

On the evening of 10 September 1960 (Pagume 5, 1952 in the Ethiopian calendar), a member of Emperor Haile Selassie's Imperial Bodyguard named Abebe Bikila (አበበ ቢቂላ) won the Olympic marathon in Rome, running barefoot. [Olympedia: Abebe Bikila](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) His time of 2:15:16.2 was a world best, and he became the first Black African to win an Olympic marathon gold medal. [World Athletics: Remembering Bikila's 1960 Olympic marathon victory on its 60th anniversary](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory) Four years later in Tokyo he became the first person to win the Olympic marathon twice in a row, again in world-best time. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) A car crash in 1969 left him paralysed, and he died in 1973 at 41. World Athletics credits him with making possible the East African distance-running tradition that followed. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory)

## From Jato to the Imperial Bodyguard

Abebe was born on 7 August 1932 in Jato, in today's Amhara region. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) Britannica gives his birthplace as Mont. [Britannica: Abebe Bikila](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Abebe-Bikila) World Athletics notes that he was born on the same day that Argentina's Juan Zabala won the 1932 Olympic marathon in Los Angeles. He grew up herding cattle. On a visit to Addis Ababa as a teenager he watched the Imperial Bodyguard training at the palace, and at 19 he applied to join and was accepted. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory)

The guards were encouraged to exercise hard, and Abebe turned out to be a natural runner. He started at 5,000 and 10,000 meters and ran his first marathon at the 1959 Military Forces Day celebrations in Addis Ababa. The Swedish coach Onni Niskanen, then Ethiopia's Director of Athletics, gave him a structured training program. One month before the Rome Olympics, Abebe won the Ethiopian marathon trial in Addis Ababa, at an altitude of more than 2,400 meters, in 2:21:23, faster than the Olympic record of the time. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory)

## Rome, 1960

Under Niskanen, Abebe had trained both with and without shoes. In the last days before the Games his shoes fell apart. A new pair bought in a Rome shop gave him blisters, so he chose to race barefoot. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory)

The race started at 5:30 p.m. to avoid the worst of the heat and was scheduled to finish after dark; hundreds of Italian soldiers lit the course with torches. Sixty-nine runners from 35 countries started at the Piazza del Campidoglio in a temperature of 23°C. The favorite was the Soviet world-record holder Sergei Popov. By 20 kilometers, Abebe and the Moroccan Rhadi Ben Abdesselam, who had finished 14th in the 10,000 meters two days earlier, had broken away together. They passed 25 kilometers in 1:20:27 and were still side by side at 35 kilometers. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory)

The decisive move came about 500 meters from the finish, at the Piazza di Porta Capena. The square held the Obelisk of Axum, which Italy had taken from Ethiopia after its invasion in 1936. World Athletics calls it a "delicious irony" that Abebe attacked at that spot. He finished at the Arch of Constantine in 2:15:16.2, a world and Olympic record 0.8 seconds faster than Popov's. Rhadi took silver about 25 seconds behind, and New Zealand's Barry Magee took bronze. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory)

## Tokyo, 1964

In Tokyo on 21 October 1964 Abebe ran in shoes and won again, in a world best of 2:12:11.2. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) His winning margin was more than four minutes, and he had had his appendix removed only 40 days before the race. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory) He carried Ethiopia's flag at the closing ceremonies in both Rome and Tokyo, and at the opening ceremony in Tokyo, and he was named BBC World Sport Star of the Year for 1964. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392)

Between 1960 and 1966 Abebe won 12 of the 13 marathons he completed. His only defeat was at the 1963 Boston Marathon, where he finished fifth. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory) He went to the 1968 Mexico City Olympics hoping for a third title but, still troubled by an injury from 1967, dropped out after about 17 kilometers. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory)

## Accident, Stoke Mandeville and Death

Late in 1969 Abebe was badly hurt in a car accident, which broke his neck and damaged his spinal cord. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory) Olympedia records that he was at first paralysed in all four limbs and that, after treatment at Stoke Mandeville Hospital in England, his condition improved to paraplegia. He then competed as an archer at the Stoke Mandeville Games, the forerunner of the Paralympics. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) Olympedia dates that competition to 1969; other accounts place it in 1970.

He died in Addis Ababa on 25 October 1973 (Tikimt 15, 1966 E.C.), aged 41. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) World Athletics gives the cause as a brain haemorrhage related to the accident. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory) Thousands attended his funeral. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) The International Olympic Committee later awarded him the Olympic Order in silver, in 1984. [Olympedia](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67392) The journalist Tim Judah's biography, _Bikila: Ethiopia's Barefoot Olympian_, was published in 2008. [WorldCat: Bikila: Ethiopia's Barefoot Olympian](https://search.worldcat.org/oclc/310218562)

## The Runners Who Followed

Abebe's countryman Mamo Wolde, who had first run at the Olympics in 1956, succeeded him as Olympic marathon champion in Mexico City in 1968, after also taking silver in the 10,000 meters there. He added a marathon bronze in Munich in 1972. [Olympedia: Mamo Wolde](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67434)

Miruts Yifter, discovered as a runner after he joined the Ethiopian Air Force, won 10,000-meter bronze in Munich in 1972. He missed the 1976 Montreal Games when Ethiopia boycotted them, then won both the 5,000 and the 10,000 meters at the 1980 Moscow Olympics with his finishing sprint. [Olympedia: Miruts Yifter](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67440)

In 1992 Derartu Tulu won the 10,000 meters in Barcelona and became the first Black African woman to win an Olympic gold medal. She ran a victory lap with the runner-up, Elana Meyer of newly readmitted South Africa, in what was seen as a landmark moment, and she won the event again in 2000. [Olympedia: Derartu Tulu](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/67372) World Athletics names later Ethiopian stars such as Haile Gebrselassie, Kenenisa Bekele and Dibaba among the East African runners who "all owe a debt of gratitude" to Abebe. [World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/news/heritage/abebe-bikila-1960-olympic-marathon-victory)

The obelisk Abebe passed in Rome had been taken during Italy's occupation. Ethiopia's earlier victory over Italy is covered in [the Battle of Adwa](/en/history/battle-of-adwa), and the emperor and empress who led it in [Menelik II and Taytu Betul](/en/people/menelik-ii-and-taytu-betul).

# Abebe Bikila and the Marathon Legacy: The Barefoot Pioneer of African Athletics

On the torchlit evening of September 10, 1960, during the Rome Olympic Games, a transformative moment unfolded in the annals of world sport. [Judah: Bikila: Ethiopia's Barefoot Olympian](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/bikila-ethiopias-barefoot-olympian-tim-judah) Abebe Bikila (1932–1973), an unheralded member of the Ethiopian Imperial Bodyguard from the rural village of Jato, ran the grueling 42.195-kilometer marathon **barefoot** through the historic streets of the former colonial capital. He shattered the world record in 2:15:16.2 to become the first Black African in history to win an Olympic gold medal. [Olympics: Abebe Bikila](https://olympics.com/en/athletes/abebe-bikila)

## The Symbolism of Rome 1960

Bikila’s decision to run without shoes arose purely from practical comfort: the newly issued running shoes provided by the Olympic supplier caused severe blisters during warm-ups, prompting him to race barefoot as he had during highland training back home under Swedish-Finnish coach Onni Niskanen.

The marathon course held profound historical and symbolic weight:

- It wound along the ancient Appian Way, passing within sight of the 1,700-year-old Axum Obelisk—which Italian fascist forces had looted from northern Ethiopia in 1937 and erected in Rome as a war trophy.
- Breaking away from Moroccan runner Rhadi Ben Abdesselam precisely at the obelisk, Bikila surged past the Arch of Constantine to cross the finish line in darkness.

Asked why he ran without shoes, Bikila delivered his immortal reply: _"I wanted the world to know that my country, Ethiopia, has always won with determination and heroism."_ Coming exactly 25 years after Mussolini's invasion of Ethiopia, the triumph stood as a monumental symbolic vindication of African sovereignty. ## Tokyo 1964: Overcoming Adversity

Four years later at the Tokyo 1964 Olympics, Bikila achieved an even more extraordinary athletic feat. Just forty days before the race, he was hospitalized in Addis Ababa and underwent emergency surgery for acute appendicitis.

Despite missing weeks of conditioning, he entered the marathon (this time wearing shoes) and delivered a masterclass in pacing. He pulled away from the field to win by over four minutes, breaking his own world record in 2:12:11.2 and becoming the first athlete in Olympic history to successfully defend a marathon title. [Olympics](https://olympics.com/en/athletes/abebe-bikila) Upon crossing the line, to the disbelief of 75,000 spectators at the National Stadium, he calmly engaged in calisthenics on the infield grass, showing zero sign of exhaustion.

## Indomitable Spirit in Later Life

In 1969, a tragic automobile accident near Sheno left Bikila quadriplegic. True to his resilient nature, he refused to abandon sport, competing in international paralympic competitions in archery, sledge-racing, and table tennis. When he passed away in 1973 at the age of 41, Emperor Haile Selassie declared an official day of national mourning, and over 65,000 mourners gathered in Addis Ababa to honor their national hero.

## The Dynasty He Built

Abebe Bikila’s barefoot steps ignited the East African distance-running revolution. Following directly in his footsteps:

- **Mamo Wolde**: Olympic marathon champion at Mexico City 1968.
- **Miruts Yifter**: Double 5,000m and 10,000m gold medalist at Moscow 1980 ("Yifter the Shifter").
- **Derartu Tulu**: First Black African woman Olympic champion (Barcelona 1992 10,000m).
- Modern titans including **Haile Gebrselassie**, **Kenenisa Bekele**, **Tirunesh Dibaba**, and **Gudaf Tsegay**, cementing Ethiopia's standing as a global athletic powerhouse.

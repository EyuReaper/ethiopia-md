# Ge’ez Illuminations and Modern Art: From Parchment Icons to Modern Masters

Ethiopia has one of the longest continuous painting traditions in Africa, and for most of its history that tradition belonged to the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. Its earliest surviving works are the Garima Gospels, two illustrated gospel books kept at the monastery of Abba Garima in northern Ethiopia, which radiocarbon testing at Oxford dated to between 330 and 650 CE. [Medievalists.net: Garima Gospels found to be oldest surviving Christian illustrated manuscripts](https://www.medievalists.net/2010/07/garima-gospels-found-to-be-oldest-surviving-christian-illustrated-manuscripts/) The same visual language later covered church walls and ceilings, such as the angel ceiling of Debre Berhan Selassie in Gondar. In the 20th century, artists trained at home and abroad turned it into a modern art scene with an art school in Addis Ababa, works in international museums and one of the best-known public artworks on the continent.

## The Garima Gospels

The Garima Gospels were first reported on in the 1950s, but scholars were able to examine and conserve them only from 2006, in a project run by the British-based Ethiopian Heritage Fund. [Medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2010/07/garima-gospels-found-to-be-oldest-surviving-christian-illustrated-manuscripts/) Two parchment fragments taken for testing gave a date range of 330 to 650 CE. Before that, the books had been thought to be no older than the 12th century. [Medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2010/07/garima-gospels-found-to-be-oldest-surviving-christian-illustrated-manuscripts/)

Tradition holds that Abba Garima brought the manuscripts from Constantinople in 494; this is a legend, and the French specialist Jacques Mercier has suggested a date of around 600 instead. [Medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2010/07/garima-gospels-found-to-be-oldest-surviving-christian-illustrated-manuscripts/) The pages contain vivid illustrations in an early Byzantine style, including a depiction of the Temple in Jerusalem, a portrait of Saint Luke and images of more than twenty kinds of bird. Because they were kept dry and in the dark, their colors still look fresh. The conservation work was done inside the monastery, since the books were not allowed to leave it, and every intervention was designed to be reversible. [Medievalists.net](https://www.medievalists.net/2010/07/garima-gospels-found-to-be-oldest-surviving-christian-illustrated-manuscripts/)

## The Visual Language of Church Painting

Icons in a distinctive Ethiopian style are central to the visual culture of Ethiopian Orthodoxy, depicting Ethiopian and universal saints alongside biblical scenes. [Catholics & Cultures: Ethiopian icons feature saints and biblical scenes](https://www.catholicsandcultures.org/ethiopia/visual-culture) The style makes moral status easy to read. With few exceptions, evil figures are shown in profile with only one eye visible, while holy figures are always shown with both eyes. The rule is not absolute: the devil himself is often shown with both eyes, although his evil character is obvious from the rest of the image. [Catholics & Cultures](https://www.catholicsandcultures.org/ethiopia/visual-culture)

The best-known example of this tradition on a large scale is Debre Berhan Selassie church in Gondar, one of the components of the Fasil Ghebbi World Heritage Site. Its walls and ceilings are covered with murals, and the ceiling in particular is filled with the faces of winged cherubic angels set between wooden beams. [ICCROM: Detail of angel ceiling, Debre Berhan Selassie Church, Gondar](https://www.iccrom.org/resources/resources-of-the-month/ethiopia-detail-angel-ceiling-debre-berhan-selassie-church-gondar) The paintings have needed professional care. ICCROM, the intergovernmental conservation body, sent missions to the church from 1973, which led to three campaigns between 1979 and 1982 to preserve and restore the wall paintings, with support from the Ethiopian government and UNESCO. [ICCROM](https://www.iccrom.org/resources/resources-of-the-month/ethiopia-detail-angel-ceiling-debre-berhan-selassie-church-gondar) Published accounts of when the ceiling was painted and how many faces it holds disagree, so those figures are left out here.

## An Art School for Addis Ababa

Modern art education in Ethiopia began with a single school. According to the sculptor and Alle School teacher Bekele Mekonnen, the Addis Ababa Fine Art School was founded under the Department of Education and Fine Arts and inaugurated by Emperor Haile Selassie on 23 July 1958 (Hamle 16, 1950 E.C.), the emperor's birthday. [OnCurating: Reshaping Wax: The Alle School of Fine Arts and Design](https://on-curating.org/issue-49-reader/reshaping-wax-addis-ababathe-alle-school-of-fine-arts-and-design.html) The driving force was Ale Felege Selam, a painter who had graduated from the Art Institute of Chicago in the early 1950s. He became the head of the school and, at first, its only Ethiopian teacher; the rest of the faculty were Europeans living in Addis Ababa. Fifty years later the school was renamed in his honor, and it is now the Alle School of Fine Arts and Design of Addis Ababa University. [OnCurating](https://on-curating.org/issue-49-reader/reshaping-wax-addis-ababathe-alle-school-of-fine-arts-and-design.html)

## Afewerk Tekle (1932–2012)

Afewerk Tekle was born in Ankober on 22 October 1932 and grew up while Ethiopia was fighting the Italian occupation; Tadias links that experience to his wish to help rebuild the country. [Tadias: In Memory of Maitre Artiste Afewerk Tekle](https://www.tadias.com/04/11/2012/in-memory-of-maitre-artiste-afewerk-tekle-his-life-odyssey/) After training abroad he held a one-man exhibition at the Municipality Hall in Addis Ababa in 1954, then toured Italy, France, Spain, Portugal and Greece, studying collections of Ethiopian illustrated manuscripts and learning stained-glass technique. Back home he made religious art for St. George's Cathedral and some of the first sculptures of Ethiopian national heroes, and his designs appeared on stamps. [Tadias](https://www.tadias.com/04/11/2012/in-memory-of-maitre-artiste-afewerk-tekle-his-life-odyssey/)

His best-known work is the stained glass of Africa Hall, the headquarters of the UN Economic Commission for Africa in Addis Ababa. The building, designed by the Italian architect Arturo Mezzedimi and commissioned by Haile Selassie, was completed in 1961, and the Organisation of African Unity was founded inside it in 1963. [The Art Newspaper: Ethiopia's Africa Hall wins modernist conservation award](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/01/22/ethiopia-africa-hall-wins-modernist-conservation-award) Tekle's window, _The Total Liberation of Africa_, is a triptych of about 150 square meters divided into three panels: "Africa Then", "Africa Then and Now" and "Africa Now and in the Future". [CNN via Yahoo News: Gifted by an emperor, Ethiopia's iconic Africa Hall has been renovated](https://www.yahoo.com/news/gifted-emperor-ethiopia-iconic-africa-091612472.html) Its images include a knight bearing the UN emblem, a dragon and the grim reaper, which the restoration architect reads as a story of slaying the demons of colonization. [CNN via Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/gifted-emperor-ethiopia-iconic-africa-091612472.html) A ten-year, US$57 million restoration of the building began in 2014 and included replacing all the pieces of the stained glass. [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/01/22/ethiopia-africa-hall-wins-modernist-conservation-award) Afewerk died in April 2012 and was buried at Holy Trinity Cathedral in Addis Ababa. [Tadias](https://www.tadias.com/04/11/2012/in-memory-of-maitre-artiste-afewerk-tekle-his-life-odyssey/) Richard and Rita Pankhurst wrote his scholarly obituary. [Aethiopica: In memoriam Afäwärq Täkle (1932–2012)](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)

## Skunder Boghossian (1937–2003)

Alexander "Skunder" Boghossian took a different path. Born in Addis Ababa in 1937, he won an imperial scholarship at 17 to study at St. Martin's School of Art in London, then moved to Paris, where he studied and taught at the Académie de la Grande Chaumière and cited Frantz Fanon, Aimé Césaire and Cheikh Anta Diop among his influences. [AllAfrica: Pioneer Artist Skunder Boghossian Dies in Washington, DC](https://allafrica.com/stories/200305051135.html) In 1963 he became the first Ethiopian painter whose work was bought by the Musée d'Art Moderne in Paris, and in 1965 the first contemporary African artist whose work was purchased by the Museum of Modern Art in New York. [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/200305051135.html)

He later taught at Howard University in Washington, D.C., and in Ethiopia, and is widely described as the patriarch of a generation of Ethiopian artists. [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/200305051135.html) Jazz was a constant presence in his studio; he called it "a combination of geniuses" and "the one thing we have, black folks, as artists". [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/200305051135.html) He died in Washington in May 2003, at 66. [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/200305051135.html)

## Two Directions From One Tradition

The two best-known modern masters point in different directions. Afewerk made large public works for the imperial state and for pan-African institutions, and studied the manuscript tradition directly. [Tadias](https://www.tadias.com/04/11/2012/in-memory-of-maitre-artiste-afewerk-tekle-his-life-odyssey/) Skunder built his career in Paris and Washington, in conversation with diaspora artists and thinkers. [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/200305051135.html) Both drew on a visual culture that reached back through church murals to manuscripts like the Garima Gospels, and the art school founded in 1958 trained the generations that followed them. [OnCurating](https://on-curating.org/issue-49-reader/reshaping-wax-addis-ababathe-alle-school-of-fine-arts-and-design.html)

The church painting tradition is also preserved at Bete Maryam in Lalibela; see [the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela](/en/art/lalibela-rock-hewn-churches). Jazz, which shaped Skunder's work, took its own Ethiopian form in [Ethio-jazz and Mulatu Astatke](/en/music/ethio-jazz-mulatu-astatke).

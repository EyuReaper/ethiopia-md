# Afewerk Tekle: Painter of Modern Ethiopia and Africa Hall

## 30-second overview

Afewerk Tekle (አፈወርቅ ተክሌ, 1932 to 2012) is the artist most often called Ethiopia's greatest modern painter. His obituary in the journal _Aethiopica_ calls him "Ethiopia's most celebrated artist" ([Pankhurst and Pankhurst: In memoriam Afäwärq Täkle, Aethiopica 15](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)). Trained in London, he returned in 1954 and worked in painting, stained glass, mosaic, sculpture and design under three successive governments. His best-known work is a stained-glass triptych at Africa Hall, the Addis Ababa headquarters of the UN Economic Commission for Africa. This article covers his life, his main works, and the questions that remain about his reputation.

## Early Life and Training

Afewerk was born in Ankober on 22 October 1932, the son of a minor court official, and as a small child was seriously wounded during the Italian invasion, according to the _Aethiopica_ obituary by Richard and Rita Pankhurst. He was enrolled in the Patriots' School in Addis Ababa and then in the elite Haile Selassie I Secondary School at Kotebe. In 1947, aged 15, he was sent to Britain; the Ethiopian government had intended him to study mining engineering, but the suffragette Sylvia Pankhurst, who had seen his sketches in 1944, wrote in support of a change to art. He attended the Central School of Arts and Crafts in London and then, the obituary says, became the first African student admitted to the Slade School of Fine Art ([Aethiopica](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)). The Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism likewise describes him as introducing Western painting and sculpture techniques to Ethiopia ([Cantone: Tekle, Afewerk (1932-2012), Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://rem.routledge.com/articles/tekle-afewerk-1932-2012)).

## Return to Addis Ababa

Back in Ethiopia in 1954, he was attached to the National Library, given a studio, and held his first one-man exhibition at the Addis Ababa Municipality, opened by the emperor. The obituary says it raised the prestige and the prices of modern Ethiopian art ([Aethiopica](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)). A biography by Richard Pankhurst records that his 1954 _Crucifixion_ departed sharply from traditional church painting and prompted protests from Orthodox church leaders, and that Haile Selassie bought two paintings while seven others were returned unpaid ([Pankhurst: Maitre Artist Afewerk Tekle, Together We Learn Ethiopia](https://twlethiopia.org/article/maitre-artist-afewerk-tekle/)). That source is warmly admiring, and its date is not stated, so it works better for anecdote than for assessment.

Afterwards he and a more experienced traditional painter were assigned to decorate St. George's Cathedral, which had been damaged in 1937. Afewerk designed mosaics for an inner wall and painted a large picture of the Queen of Sheba arriving at King Solomon's court ([Aethiopica](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)).

## Africa Hall

In 1958, according to the obituary, he was commissioned to decorate Africa Hall, the new UN Economic Commission for Africa building. The result was his most famous work: a triptych of three stained-glass windows depicting "the sorrow of the colonial past, the struggle of the present, and the hope for the future of independent Africa." The authors add that he was irritated when the commission cut a door into one window to link a new building ([Aethiopica](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)). Wikipedia gives the title as _Total Liberation of Africa_ and the area as about 150 square meters ([Wikipedia: Africa Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Hall)); sources differ on whether the window dates from 1958 or 1961, since the building itself was completed in February 1961. The hall was refurbished between 2014 and 2024, and a 2024 completion year is listed by the architects' project page on ArchDaily ([ArchDaily: Africa Hall Cultural Center](https://www.archdaily.com/1023551/africa-hall-cultural-center-architectus-conrad-gargett)). That page does not mention the stained glass, so we cannot confirm from it how the window was treated in the restoration.

## Themes and Other Works

The obituary lists many other works: an equestrian bronze of Ras Makonnen in Harar, designed in 1958 and cast in 1968; stained-glass windows of four great Ethiopian warriors for the Military Academy; a _Last Judgment_ for the Catholic cathedral in Adigrat; and a large silver cross designed in 1959 for the chapel of the Tower of London. He also designed postage stamps, posters and playing cards, and painted landscapes of the Simien Mountains and Harar. Many works dealt with the African struggle for independence, such as a 1960 monochrome tribute to Patrice Lumumba, and a 1969 painting that placed the Aksum obelisk among symbols of African civilization ([Aethiopica](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)). The Routledge entry describes his work as centered on Pan-African and Christian themes and the optimism of the 1950s liberation movements ([Routledge](https://rem.routledge.com/articles/tekle-afewerk-1932-2012)).

He also painted in a darker mode. In 1972, during the unrest before the revolution, the obituary notes, he turned to more somber realistic paintings such as _Sick at Heart_ and _The Peasant_ ([Aethiopica](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)).

## Honors

The Pankhursts record that in 1964 he was the first winner of the Haile Selassie I Prize for Fine Arts and that his self-portrait became the first work by an African artist in the permanent collection of the Uffizi Gallery in Florence ([Aethiopica](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)). Other lists of prizes, such as a 1977 gold medal in Algiers and membership of the Russian Academy of Arts, appear in the Wikipedia biography, which we use only as a secondary check ([Wikipedia: Afewerk Tekle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afewerk_Tekle)). The Pankhurst biography cites more than 82 awards, an unreferenced count.

## Villa Alpha and Death

He designed and built his house and studio, Villa Alpha, "inspired by the Gondär Castles," with a private chapel, and intended to leave it to Ethiopia as a museum; for this reason he was reluctant to sell originals and produced reproductions instead ([Aethiopica](https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/aethiopica/article/view/674)). He died on 10 April 2012, and his funeral was held at Holy Trinity Cathedral on 13 April. The Pankhursts, who were close friends, describe a flamboyant, punctual and sometimes quarrelsome personality.

## What We Cannot Settle

Most biographical material is by admirers or friends, including the main source here. Judgments about the artistic merit of his work, and about how far his official favor under the monarchy and the Derg shaped his reputation, are rarely argued in the sources we could access. The obituary also records that some critics saw the influence of El Greco, which he did not appreciate. Dates for individual works differ between sources, and we have flagged them.

## Closing

Afewerk Tekle gave modern Ethiopian art an international face and linked it to Pan-African politics. The best next step for this article is a second, independent critical assessment of his work.

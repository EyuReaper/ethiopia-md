# Coffee: Ethiopia's Export Economy

## 30-second overview

Coffee is the crop most closely tied to Ethiopia's identity, and it is also the country's leading agricultural export. The Ethiopian Coffee and Tea Authority (ECTA) reported record export revenue of 2.653 billion US dollars for the 2024/25 fiscal year, from 468,967 tonnes ([Food Business MEA: Ethiopia's coffee exports hit record US$2.65B](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopias-coffee-exports-hit-record-us2-65b-in-2024-25-fiscal-year/)), and a state briefing reported by Xinhua put 2025/26 at 3 billion dollars ([Xinhua via The Star: Ethiopia earns record 3 bln USD from annual coffee exports](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/07/05/ethiopia-earns-record-3-bln-usd-from-annual-coffee-exports)). Most of the crop is grown by smallholders. The numbers differ by source and by definition of the year, so this article gives each figure with its origin. For the cultural side of coffee, see [Ethiopian Coffee Culture](/en/culture/ethiopian-coffee-culture).

## Where Arabica Comes From

A 2020 study in _Scientific Reports_, led by World Coffee Research with the Italian genomics institute IGA and the French agricultural research institute CIRAD, concluded that arabica originated in the wild upland forests of Ethiopia. All cultivated arabica traces to a single natural cross between two other coffee species roughly 10,000 to 20,000 years ago, and the study identified two Ethiopian genetic groups, one from the Jimma-Bonga region and one around Sheka. It also found that arabica has lower genetic diversity than any other major crop species ([World Coffee Research: Study: All Arabica derived from a single ancestral plant](https://worldcoffeeresearch.org/news/2020/study-all-arabica-derived-from-a-single-ancestral-plant); [Scalabrin et al., Scientific Reports, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-61216-7)). The date of the cross is a model-based estimate; other molecular-clock analyses reported elsewhere give far older dates, which we have not verified.

## Production

The US Department of Agriculture's Foreign Agricultural Service, as summarized by Daily Coffee News in June 2026, estimates production at 11.46 million 60-kilogram bags in 2024/25 and 11.56 million in 2025/26, with a forecast of 12.1 million for 2026/27 on about 800,000 harvested hectares. Smallholders produce about 90 percent of output, and arabica predominates ([Daily Coffee News: Ethiopia coffee report, production up, government promoting modernization](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/06/12/ethiopia-coffee-report-production-up-government-promoting-modernization/)).

The Xinhua report is much higher: it says annual production rose from 500,000 tonnes five years ago to 1.5 million tonnes, and yield from 600 to 1,000 kilograms per hectare, according to the ECTA director general ([Xinhua via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/07/05/ethiopia-earns-record-3-bln-usd-from-annual-coffee-exports)). By our arithmetic, 11.56 million bags of 60 kilograms is about 694,000 tonnes, less than half the Xinhua figure. The gap may reflect different definitions, such as cherry versus green coffee, but neither source explains it, so readers should not combine them.

## Exports and Revenue

Several export figures circulate:

([Daily Coffee News, 2026](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/06/12/ethiopia-coffee-report-production-up-government-promoting-modernization/); [Daily Coffee News, 2025](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2025/06/09/ethiopia-coffee-report-production-consumption-and-exports-all-up); [Food Business MEA](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopias-coffee-exports-hit-record-us2-65b-in-2024-25-fiscal-year/); [Xinhua via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/07/05/ethiopia-earns-record-3-bln-usd-from-annual-coffee-exports))

The two 2024/25 values, 2.89 and 2.653 billion dollars, are not contradictory: the USDA uses an October-to-September marketing year, while the ECTA reports on the Ethiopian fiscal year ending in July. ECTA said volume reached 144 percent and revenue 147 percent of its targets, and its director general called it "a remarkable achievement" with "significant untapped potential" ahead ([Food Business MEA](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopias-coffee-exports-hit-record-us2-65b-in-2024-25-fiscal-year/)).

The Xinhua report says Ethiopian coffee sold at about 7,500 dollars per tonne against 6,000 the year before, while the global price fell to 2.4 dollars per kilogram from 4. It lists Saudi Arabia, Germany and China as the leading destinations, and the authority expects annual revenue of up to 6 billion dollars within five years ([Xinhua via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/07/05/ethiopia-earns-record-3-bln-usd-from-annual-coffee-exports)). The five-year projection is an official aim, not a forecast we can verify. The USDA account puts exports to China at 670,000 bags in 2024/25, up 264 percent ([Daily Coffee News, 2026](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/06/12/ethiopia-coffee-report-production-up-government-promoting-modernization/)).

## Livelihoods

Estimates of the number of coffee-dependent people vary. A 2021 research report by the International Institute for Environment and Development says coffee supports over 5 million smallholder farmers and that smallholders sit "at the bottom of a long value chain" ([IIED: Ethiopian coffee marketing reforms and smallholder coffee producers](https://www.iied.org/node/73331)). A report by the Food and Land Use Coalition, prepared with the ECTA and funded by Norway, says 25 percent of the population depends on coffee, including more than 6 million smallholders, and that more than 500,000 hectares were renovated in five years. It lists aging trees, declining productivity, climate change, pests and limited long-term finance as challenges ([FOLU: Coffee Production in Ethiopia](https://www.foodandlandusecoalition.org/knowledge-hub/coffee-production-in-ethiopia-a-call-for-renovation-and-rejuvenation/)). The page's date is unclear, so we treat these as recent but unverified figures.

## Policy Changes

The USDA account notes several policy shifts: 100,000 hectares allocated for private-sector coffee development, a national traceability system being built to meet European Union deforestation rules, tighter financial requirements for exporters, and permission for vetted foreign companies to export raw coffee directly ([Daily Coffee News, 2026](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/06/12/ethiopia-coffee-report-production-up-government-promoting-modernization/)). The long-running question of how coffee is sold domestically is covered in [The Ethiopian Commodity Exchange](/en/economy/ethiopia-commodity-exchange). IIED notes that trading outside the exchange was allowed again after reforms in 2017 ([IIED](https://www.iied.org/node/73331)).

## Open Questions

Three points remain unsettled in the sources we could read: the true production volume, how much of the export price reaches farmers, and whether record revenue reflects higher prices or volume. The Xinhua report suggests price played a large part in 2025/26, while the USDA reports a volume jump in 2024/25. None of the cited sources gives the farmers' share of the export price for the recent years.

## Closing

Coffee ties Ethiopia's highland farms to world markets, and the recent record figures show both the opportunity and the measurement problems. A fuller article would add an independent source on farm-gate prices and on the share of exports that coffee represents.

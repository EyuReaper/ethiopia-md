# Ethiopian Airlines: Africa’s Global Aviation Powerhouse and Transit Crossroads

Founded on December 21, 1945, by Emperor Haile Selassie in partnership with Trans World Airlines (TWA), **Ethiopian Airlines** has developed into one of the most remarkable corporate success stories in modern Africa. [CAPA: Ethiopian Airlines Profile](https://centreforaviation.com/) Operating as the continent’s largest, most technologically advanced, and consistently profitable carrier, the state-owned airline has transformed Addis Ababa’s Bole International Airport into the undisputed aviation crossroads of Africa. [IATA: Airline Passenger Market Analysis](https://www.iata.org/)

## Early Foundations and Indigenous Professionalization

Commencing scheduled commercial operations in April 1946 with five surplus Douglas C-47 Skytrains flying to Cairo, the airline embarked on an aggressive policy of rapid Ethiopianization:

- By 1971, all expatriate flight crews and administrative positions had been fully transitioned to Ethiopian personnel.
- The carrier established its own maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) facilities and founded the **Ethiopian Aviation University**, training pilots, aircraft technicians, and cabin crews for dozens of African and Middle Eastern nations. [Ethiopian Airlines: History](https://www.ethiopianairlines.com/about-us/our-history)

Today, Ethiopian Airlines operates Africa’s youngest and modern fleet of over 145 aircraft, having served as the launch customer on the continent for the Boeing 787 Dreamliner and the Airbus A350.

## The Global Route Network and Multi-Hub Strategy

From its central geographic hub in Addis Ababa, Ethiopian Airlines serves more than 135 international passenger and cargo destinations across five continents:

- Direct, non-stop long-haul connectivity linking Africa to major commercial capitals in North America, South America, Europe, the Middle East, and East Asia.
- **Pan-African Multi-Hub Network**: Rather than centralizing all operations in Addis Ababa, the carrier established equity-stake secondary hubs: **ASKY Airlines** in Lomé (Togo) serving West Africa, **Malawi Airlines** in Lilongwe for Southern Africa, and **Zambia Airways** in Lusaka.

This hub architecture has allowed Ethiopian Airlines to overtake Dubai and European legacy carriers as the dominant transfer nexus for long-haul passenger traffic entering Africa.

## Cargo Expansion and Pandemic Agility

During the COVID-19 pandemic, when global commercial passenger travel collapsed by over 90%, Ethiopian Airlines demonstrated agile corporate resilience. The airline stripped passenger seats from 25 passenger Boeing 777 and 787 airliners to create temporary "preighters" (passenger freighters). Operating around the clock, Ethiopian Cargo transported hundreds of millions of doses of vaccines, PPE, and critical medical supplies between China, Europe, the Americas, and 54 African nations, sustaining profitability while global competitors incurred historic losses.

## Vision 2035 and the Bishoftu Mega-Airport

Under its ambitious corporate roadmap **"Vision 2035,"** Ethiopian Airlines plans to expand its fleet to over 270 aircraft, connect over 200 international destinations, and generate $25 billion in annual revenue.

To overcome capacity limits at Bole International Airport, the airline is developing a massive greenfield **Mega-Airport City in Bishoftu** (45 km south of Addis Ababa). Designed with a planned capacity of 100 million passengers annually across four parallel runways, the project will represent the largest airport infrastructure development in African history.

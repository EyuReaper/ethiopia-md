## Principle

**Translate for meaning, not word-for-word.** Read as if a native English speaker who knows Ethiopia wrote it directly, not as a mechanical rendering of the Amharic source.

## Sensitive Topics — Handle with Care

Topics needing extra care in translation:

- **Ethnic federalism and inter-ethnic relations**: Ethiopia's constitution organizes the federation around ethnic/linguistic "Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples." Translate descriptions of this structure accurately without editorializing about it.
- **The Derg era (1974–1987) and the imperial period before it**: keep historical framing factual and sourced; avoid loaded language for either the monarchy or the military government.
- **Recent/ongoing conflicts**: per EDITORIAL.md's "Perspectives System," present multiple well-sourced viewpoints rather than a single narrative, and prefer well-established encyclopedic framing over developing news framing.
- **Avoid outdated/othering language**: no "tribe" (prefer "ethnic group"), no "primitive," no "third world," no treating any one ethnic group's culture as representative of "Ethiopian culture" as a whole.

## Tone

- Prefer plain, encyclopedic English (AP-style leanings) over florid description
- Conversational is fine where the Amharic source is conversational — don't over-formalize
- Avoid generic AI-slop adjectives ("unique," "vibrant," "rich") unless backed by a specific, sourced detail

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Using "Abyssinia" as an unmarked synonym for the modern state of Ethiopia
- ❌ Treating one ethnic group's or region's traditions as generically "Ethiopian" without qualification
- ❌ Silently converting Ethiopian-calendar dates to Gregorian without noting the conversion
- ❌ Using "tribe"/"tribal" or "primitive" without a specific, sourced, non-othering reason

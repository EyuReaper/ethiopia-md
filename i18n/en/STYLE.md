# English style guide

This guide applies to English translations in `knowledge/en/`. For the editorial rules that apply to every language, see [`EDITORIAL.md`](../../EDITORIAL.md).

## Voice

- Write for an intelligent reader who may know nothing about Ethiopia. Explain terms the first time they appear.
- Use plain, active sentences. Prefer "Emperor Menelik II led the army" to "The army was led by Emperor Menelik II" unless the passive is clearer.
- Keep the author's point of view and emotional tone from the Amharic original. Do not add opinions the original does not express.

## Terms

- Give Ethiopian words in their transliterated form, italicised on first use, with a short gloss: _injera_, a fermented flatbread made from teff.
- Use the transliteration of the Amharic file. Where English sources spell a name differently, use the spelling in the Amharic file and note the variant once.
- Use the English name of an institution when there is an official one. Otherwise give the Amharic name in transliteration.
- Keep the Amharic script for a name only where it helps the reader, for example in a glossary or the first mention of a term.

## Dates and numbers

- Dates in the body are written as day, month, year (for example, 2 May 2026) when the day is known. Give the Gregorian date first when an Ethiopian calendar date is used, with the Ethiopian date in brackets.
- Frontmatter dates are always `YYYY-MM-DD`.
- Use numerals for numbers above nine, and always give units and years for figures.
- Thousands use commas: 10,000. Decimals use a point.

## Sources

- Keep every citation from the original, with the same URL.
- Do not cite a source you have not opened.
- Link text is the source's title, not "click here".

## Formatting

- Headings use sentence case: "Economy and trade", not "Economy And Trade".
- Use paragraphs for explanation. Lists only for list-shaped material.
- Use wikilinks in the same form as the Amharic file. They resolve by title, so the English title must match an existing published article.

## Checks before submitting

- Every fact and citation in the Amharic file appears in the English file.
- The text reads naturally in English and does not follow the Amharic sentence order.
- No English word is left untranslated without a gloss.
